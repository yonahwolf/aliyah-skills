# Job Boards, Recruiters, Communities, and Networking

Last verified: November 2026 (site features change; confirm availability yourself).

## Job boards and where jobs actually appear

| Source | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn (linkedin.com/jobs, set location Israel) | Tech, multinationals, English-language roles | Most useful for English speakers; recruiters search profiles; "Open to Work" settings; set alerts |
| Company career pages (many run on Comeet, also Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby) | Startups and scale-ups | Often the most current; many Israeli companies use Comeet-powered career pages (a Comeet link on the careers page is a tell); check target companies weekly |
| Wellfound (formerly AngelList Talent) | Startups, some remote | Check how many Israeli listings are current before relying on it |
| Startup Nation Central (startupnationcentral.org) | Company discovery (Finder database), some jobs | Use to build target company lists by sector and stage |
| Geektime (geektime.co.il) and Geektime jobs | Hebrew tech scene news and jobs | Hebrew-heavy |
| Drushim (drushim.co.il), AllJobs (alljobs.co.il), JobMaster (jobmaster.co.il) | Broad Israeli market, Hebrew-first; non-tech and enterprise | Many postings in Hebrew; use browser translation; filter by "English" or "ללא עברית" is unreliable |
| Nefesh B'Nefesh job board (nbn.org.il/jobboard) | Roles open to olim, English-friendly | The page advertises 1,000+ jobs from 280+ companies (as of Nov 2026); filters include hybrid, from home, internship, student |
| Ministry of Aliyah and Integration programs and Employment Service (gov.il, also the Israel Employment Service "Sherut HaTaasuka") | Public-sector and subsidized programs | Verify current offerings; Hebrew-heavy |
| University career centers (Technion, TAU, HUJI, BGU, Reichman) | Students, alumni, junior roles, job fairs | Open to enrolled students and often alumni |
| Facebook groups | Tips, referrals, apartments, olim jobs | See below |

Practical rule: boards are the weakest channel for experienced hires. Most hires come through referrals, recruiter outreach, and direct applications with a warm contact.

## Recruiters and agencies

- Israeli tech recruiting firms exist across sizes; examples reported in the press include GotFriends, Aman Group, Ethosia, and Tikal (staffing/consulting). Existence of a firm here is not a recommendation; check Glassdoor and community reviews.
- NBN Recruitment Services (Nefesh B'Nefesh): accepts CVs, interviews candidates, and says it works with employers in fields including CPA, high tech, marketing, SDR/sales, corporate law, and office management. This is the organization's own description; do not assume placement is guaranteed.
- How to work with recruiters: you do not pay them (employers do); give a salary range from research, not your last US figure; ask what stage the process is at and whether the role is exclusive to them; be wary of agencies that ask for money or "preparation fees."
- Corporate in-house recruiters at multinationals often contact people on LinkedIn; a complete English profile with the right keywords and location helps.

## Communities and meetups (tech)

- Search Meetup.com and Luma for Tel Aviv, Jerusalem, Haifa, and Beer Sheva events (cloud native, Python, JS, DevOps, AI, security).
- Tech communities to look for: GDG chapters, DevOps Days Tel Aviv, "Developers in Israel" and "Israel High Tech" groups on LinkedIn/Facebook, Olim.Tech (a community/training provider listed among NBN partners), AI Tinkerers-style meetups, women-in-tech groups, university alumni networks in Israel, and Israeli open-source or security communities. Event names change; verify they still run.
- Conferences and meetups are strongest for networking after the first two months in Israel; online events work pre-aliyah.
- Veteran-network communities (alumni of tech units) are mostly closed to non-veterans; do not try to break in, instead build parallel networks (university, olim, hobby, volunteer).

## LinkedIn for olim (pre- and post-aliyah)

1. Set location to the target Israeli city (or "Israel") and add "Making aliyah [month/year]" in the headline or About if true.
2. Add a line stating availability and work authorization: olim have the right to work without a visa.
3. Add a Hebrew section only if your Hebrew is professional; otherwise state "English (native), Hebrew (learning)".
4. Follow 40-60 target companies, join the posting alert for each, and connect with 5-10 employees per company (engineers and managers, not only recruiters).
5. Post or comment weekly on technical topics to be found.
6. Message etiquette: see "Outreach" below.

## Facebook and WhatsApp groups

Useful groups for olim include general oleh job groups, "Olim in [city]" groups, NBN-run groups, and professional groups (for example for accountants, teachers, nurses, developers). Treat job posts as leads, not guarantees; avoid sharing sensitive details; beware of scams (requests for money, unverified "visa" or "work permit" fees; olim do not need work visas). Many groups are mostly Hebrew; check the group description.

## Networking norms in Israel

- Direct and fast: people usually respond to a short, specific message and may reply on WhatsApp. Less formality than in the US.
- Informal interviews: "kafe" (coffee) meetings are common and normal; offering to come to the person is appreciated.
- Warm referrals are the main channel. An employee referral (and a bonus for them) often sends your CV to the top of the pile.
- Say what you want in the first message: a 15-minute call, a referral, or feedback on a CV.
- Follow up after 5-7 days with one polite nudge; two at most.
- Reciprocity: offer something (an introduction, a technical perspective, English editing help).

## Protektzia

Protektzia (פרוטקציה) means using personal connections to get an advantage, such as getting a CV read or an interview. In Israel it is common and not necessarily seen as corrupt in the private sector: a referral is the normal way to get seen. In public-sector hiring, formal tender procedures exist and personal connections are more constrained. For an oleh with no network, the substitute is to build one on purpose: alumni, olim organizations, synagogue/community, volunteer work, university contacts, shared friends of friends, and former colleagues who moved to Israel. Never offer money or favors in return for a position; ask for introductions and advice.

## Outreach templates (English; optional Hebrew)

Warm introduction request (to a mutual contact):
"Hi [Name], I'm planning to move to Israel in [month] and looking for [role] in [field]. I saw you know [Person] at [Company]. Would you be open to an intro? I can send a 3-line blurb to make it easy. Thank you! (תודה רבה)"

Cold LinkedIn note to an engineer or manager:
"Hi [Name], I'm a [role] with [X years] in [domain] moving to [city] in [month]. I noticed your team at [Company] works on [specific thing]. Could I ask 2-3 questions about the team and what skills matter most? 15 minutes at your convenience, I can adapt to your time zone. Thanks! (תודה)"

Recruiter note:
"Hi [Name], I'm a [role] with [stack]/[domain], arriving in Israel in [month]. I'm interested in [types of companies/roles], salary expectation roughly NIS [range, researched]. Hebrew: [level]; English: native. If you have relevant roles, I'd be glad to share my CV."

After a conversation:
"Thanks for the time today. Based on what you said about [topic], I'm applying for [role/req ID]. If you're comfortable passing my CV to [recruiter/hiring manager], I'd appreciate it. Happy to send a short blurb."

Hebrew phrases (optional): שלום (shalom, hello), תודה רבה (toda raba, thanks a lot), אשמח לשיחה קצרה (esmach le-sicha ktzara, I'd be happy for a short call), מחפש/ת עבודה בתחום ה... (mechapes/mechapeset avoda ba-tchum ha..., looking for work in the field of...). Have a Hebrew speaker check anything beyond basic greetings.

## Job fairs and events

NBN, the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration, municipalities, and universities periodically run employment fairs for olim, reservists, and graduates; announcements appear on the organizations' sites and social channels. Dates change yearly, so search for the current list rather than relying on this file.

## Temporary and student jobs

Typical bridge options for olim: English tutoring, customer support/success in English, SDR/sales for international markets, freelance writing or translation, nonprofit and fundraising roles, call-center roles in English, and part-time student jobs near campus. Check minimum wage (see `compensation-and-benefits.md`) and your rights as an employee: pay slip (teudat maskoret), pension after the qualifying period, and no cash-in-hand arrangements.
