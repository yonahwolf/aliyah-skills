# Compensation and Benefits (Israel)

Last verified: November 2026. Not tax or legal advice; consult an Israeli accountant or payroll professional. Numbers marked "reported" come from vendor/press pages and should be rechecked against the official source before use.

## Salary: how to find current ranges (never trust one source)

1. levels.fyi (Israel / Tel Aviv filters): good for larger tech companies and total compensation; sample sizes vary and skew toward high-paying firms.
2. Glassdoor and Payscale Israel pages: wide ranges, mixed quality; Payscale Israel pages exist for software engineer and R&D manager roles.
3. Israeli press: Calcalist/CTech, Globes, Geektime publish periodic salary and recruiter surveys.
4. Recruiter salary surveys and reports (e.g., GotFriends, Aman Group, Ethosia): reflect actual asks and offers; request the latest edition.
5. Zviran Compensation and Benefits Solutions with the Israel Innovation Authority publishes a tech employment/compensation survey (the Aug 2026 version covers 210 firms).
6. Peers: ask 3-5 people in similar roles, including olim; ask what is included (bonus, hishtalmut, car).
7. Government data: CBS (Central Bureau of Statistics) average wage tables, including the high-tech average. Averages are not offers.

## Reference points (dated, reported)

- National minimum wage: NIS 6,247.67 per month from 1 April 2026 (per WageIndicator, up from NIS 5,880.02). Re-verify at gov.il or the Ministry of Labor.
- Average economy-wide wage: NIS 15,223 per month in June 2026 (CBS via Globes).
- Average high-tech wage: NIS 35,760 per month in May 2026, up about 13.7% year over year (CBS via Globes). This is an average that includes senior and executive pay.
- Defense-tech recruiter survey (Globes, 29 Jul 2026), reported average monthly gross: hardware architects about NIS 50,000; algorithmic engineers about NIS 47,000; AI and VLSI engineers about NIS 45,000; experienced software developers approaching NIS 44,000. These are recruiter-survey averages for hard-to-fill roles, not entry-level.
- Entry-level and early-career figures: this skill does not state an exact starter salary because no reliable current source was verified. Use levels.fyi entry-level filters, Payscale entry-level pages, the university career center's graduate survey, and recruiter reports; expect a wide range by company type (large multinational vs small startup) and role. A reasonable verification approach is to find three offers or reports from the last 12 months and take the middle.
- Engineering management: no verified current NIS range is included here; check levels.fyi (Engineering Manager, Tel Aviv), Payscale R&D Manager/VP R&D pages, recruiter reports, and ask recruiters for ranges by company stage.

## Gross vs net

- Israeli salaries are usually quoted as gross monthly NIS ("bruto") and paid 12 times per year (some Israeli firms mention "13th salary"; clarify).
- "Global" salary (sachar globali) means overtime/benefits are bundled; "standard" means pension and other items are listed as add-ons. Ask which.
- Tax is progressive. 2026 brackets (annual, reported by Deloitte's Israel highlights): 10% up to NIS 84,120; 14% to 120,720; 20% to 228,000; 31% to 301,200; 35% to 560,280; 47% above; a 3% surtax applies above NIS 721,560, and a further 2% on capital income above that. Brackets are updated by law; the Tax Authority publishes the current table. Some reports mention proposals to widen brackets; verify.
- Tax credit points (nekudot zikuy): each reduces annual tax by a set shekel amount (reported NIS 2,904 per point per year for 2026). Residents get base points (the Tax Authority guide lists 2.5 for men and 2.75 for women). Olim get additional points: 1 point for months 1-12, 3 points for months 13-30, 2 points for months 31-42, and 1 point for months 43-54 from arrival (Tax Authority new-immigrant guide, Oct 2026). Parents of young children, discharged soldiers, and degree holders can earn additional points; check the Tax Authority calculator.
- Bituach Leumi (National Insurance) and health tax: employee and employer rates differ by income level, with a lower rate up to a threshold (reported 2026 employer rate 4.51% up to NIS 7,703/month and 7.6% above, up to the NIS 51,910 ceiling) and a corresponding employee share. See the Bituach Leumi rate table for the exact employee rates.
- Quick net estimate: use an online Israeli net-salary calculator (several free ones exist; check that they use the current year's brackets and include credit points) and cross-check with the first payslip.
- Olim should expect their first months of payroll to be messy: file Tofes 101 (employee tax form) with correct credit points and an immigrant declaration; request an Israeli tax number via the oleh process; check the payslip for correct points.

## Pension, severance, and keren hishtalmut

- Mandatory pension: employee contributes 6% of gross salary, employer contributes 6.5%, plus an employer severance component (reported as 6% mandatory, or 8.33% under a Section 14 arrangement). Pension is mandatory for employees after a qualifying period; check the Ministry of Finance Capital Markets Authority pension site. (Rates from a vendor page and general knowledge; verify with the Ministry of Labor or the pension fund.)
- Section 14 (Severance Pay Law): if the employer deposits 8.33% monthly into your pension/severance fund under a Section 14 arrangement, you generally keep that money even if you are dismissed or leave, and the employer has no additional severance liability in most circumstances. Ask whether the employment contract includes Section 14.
- Keren hishtalmut (study/training fund): typical split employer 7.5% and employee 2.5% of salary up to a ceiling. A reported ceiling for the tax-advantaged deposit base is a monthly salary of about NIS 15,712 (a figure reported by Globes and a vendor page; the ceiling is updated yearly, verify with the Tax Authority). Withdrawals are generally tax-free after six years; confirm current rules and any shorter-term exceptions. Many tech employers offer it; some small employers do not. Ask for it in negotiation.
- Managers' insurance (bituach menahalim) and pension funds are different vehicles; compare fees (dmei nihul) and track record.

## Other statutory items (reported for 2026 by a vendor, verify)

Annual leave: 16 days at the start rising with seniority (days per week schedule affects the count); sick leave accrual of 1.5 days per month; recuperation pay (dmei havraah) at a daily rate (reported NIS 451.50 per day, 5 days in the first year); severance after one year of service (about one month's salary per year if not covered by Section 14); employer reserve-duty obligations (the Bituach Leumi reimburses salary for reserve duty).

## Equity (ESOP/RSUs)

- Startups grant options; multinationals grant RSUs. Ask: number of options and the percentage of fully diluted shares, strike price, vesting schedule and cliff, acceleration on exit, post-termination exercise window, and whether the plan is under the Section 102 capital-gains track (often favorable tax if held for the required period with a trustee).
- Value options conservatively; many startups return little. Equity in a company in a down round or layoffs period may be worth far less than the paper figure.
- RSUs from foreign parents and Israeli subsidiaries are taxed under Section 102 rules if properly set up; cross-border tax interplay (US citizens) is complex; use a cross-border accountant.
- For US citizens: Israeli Section 102 treatment does not automatically map to US tax treatment; check US tax on exercise and sale.

## Other benefits

Company car (leasing or allowance) is common at some firms and is a taxable benefit (shovi ha-rechev); lunch/Cibus or 10bis meal cards; phone and equipment; commuting or parking; gym; study days; supplemental health insurance; flexible hybrid policy (typically 2-3 office days); reserve-duty top-ups; unpaid sabbatical rarely. Compare total cost of employment and net benefit; a car is not a gift if it adds taxable value.
