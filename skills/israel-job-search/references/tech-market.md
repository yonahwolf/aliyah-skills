# Israeli Tech Market (snapshot)

Last verified: November 2026. This is a snapshot; the market moves quarterly. Re-check the sources at the bottom before quoting numbers.

## How the market is doing (be candid with the user)

- Size: more than 400,000 people worked in high-tech in 2025 (about 11.4% of employees), up 2.3% on 2024, but growth has slowed sharply versus the 2013-2022 average. R&D employment fell for the first time in a decade. High-tech was about 18% of GDP and roughly 58% of exports. (Israel Innovation Authority, State of High Tech 2026)
- Funding and exits: roughly USD 14.6 billion raised in 2025 (up about 30%), fewer but larger rounds, around 70% of VC from foreign investors, and record exit value (large deals such as Wiz, CyberArk, Armis are cited). Cash-rich acquirers do not automatically mean more junior hiring.
- Employment in the first half of 2026: a survey of 210 companies (about 130,000 employees; companies over 50 staff only) found total tech employment almost flat. Hiring was about 8% of workforces, layoffs 2.8%, voluntary departures 4.3%. Software layoffs were much higher (6.6%); hardware lower (1.1%). Companies with 50-200 employees had the highest layoff rate (8.7%). Planned H2 2026 recruiting was lower than H1. About half of firms planning layoffs said AI influenced the decision, though few called it the main reason. (Globes, 11 Aug 2026, citing the Innovation Authority and Zviran)
- Government data: tech employment was roughly flat year over year in May 2026 (about 397,000 jobs per CBS via Globes), while the average tech wage was about NIS 35,760 per month, up about 13.7% year over year. Averages are skewed by senior pay and layoffs of lower-paid workers; do not treat them as a typical offer.
- Hiring abroad: demand for hiring outside Israel rose more than 23% in H1 2026 per a recruiting firm's report, tied partly to the strong shekel; Romania, Poland, Serbia, and Portugal are frequent destinations. The shekel-dollar rate is a recurring pressure on Israeli salary costs. (Ynet, Aman Group report)
- Hiring cycles have lengthened (reported from about 30 days to about 45-60). Expect a slower process than in the 2021 peak.
- Junior roles: scarce. A mid-2025 snapshot found only about 1.5% of tech vacancies open to people with no experience, and employers asking for about three years for "junior" roles. Manual QA and generic web development are eroding; AI, data, cloud, DevOps, and cybersecurity are stronger. (Globes, June 2025; Ynet 2026)
- Relative strength: defense-tech (new openings reported up about 20% year over year in 2026 per one recruiter survey), hardware/chips/embedded/FPGA/VLSI, AI/ML and data engineering, cybersecurity, cloud/infrastructure. Many defense-tech roles require Israeli citizenship and a security clearance; check each posting.
- Multinationals: 511 foreign companies have R&D operations in Israel since 2000 (35 started in 2025). Nonetheless the share of global R&D headcount located in Israel has been declining as companies add capacity in Eastern Europe and the US.

Honest takeaway: experienced engineers with relevant skills (cloud, data, AI, security, infrastructure, embedded/hardware) and a referral have good prospects; entry-level and generalist web/QA candidates face a hard market; the process is slow. Hebrew is rarely required in tech R&D but helps with team culture and non-R&D roles.

## Hubs

- Tel Aviv / Gush Dan (Tel Aviv, Ramat Gan, Givatayim, Petah Tikva, Herzliya, Ra'anana, Kfar Saba, Rehovot, Holon): the majority of openings (a 2025 snapshot put it above 80%). Startups concentrate in Tel Aviv and Ramat Gan; Herzliya Pituach and Ra'anana host many multinationals and medical/cyber firms.
- Herzliya: multinational R&D centers, cyber, adtech, medical devices.
- Haifa and the North (Matam park, Yokneam, Tirat Carmel): Intel, Apple, Nvidia (Yokneam area), chips, hardware, Technion talent. Lower rents.
- Jerusalem (Har Hotzvim, Malcha): Mobileye, Intel, biotech, medical devices, cyber; large English-speaking community; also government and academia.
- Beer Sheva (Gav-Yam Negev, cyber park): cyber and defense-adjacent firms, Ben-Gurion University, army tech units relocating to the Negev. Lower cost of living and incentives for relocating.
- Also: Be'er Ya'akov, Rehovot (Weizmann, bio), Caesarea, Netanya, Modi'in, Lod (hybrid commuting is common).

## Company types and what to expect

| Type | Typical traits | Hiring notes |
|---|---|---|
| Early startup (seed/A) | Small team, equity-heavy, fast, Hebrew-speaking teams common | CEO/CTO interviews, home assignment, referrals matter most |
| Scale-up (B-D, pre-IPO) | Structured process, some English-only teams | Standard loop, stronger comp bands, layoffs recently concentrated in 50-200 person firms |
| Multinational R&D center | Global processes, English-friendly, steadier | Recruiters use LinkedIn; leveling bands and RSUs |
| Established public Israeli tech | Mixed, large teams | Hebrew more common in internal teams |
| Defense-adjacent and defense startups | Often clearance and veteran-heavy | Citizenship/clearance filters; many founded by veterans of tech units |
| Banks, telecom, enterprise IT | Hebrew-first, lower pay in general | Stable; not the typical English-only path |

## In-demand skills (mid-2026 reports)

Data and AI engineering, cloud, DevOps/SRE, cybersecurity, integration/CRM roles (Aman Group); embedded, C++, Python, FPGA/VLSI, computer vision, sensor integration (defense-tech roles). Weaker: manual QA, generic frontend/backend without data/AI exposure, entry-level marketing/content roles for English speakers.

## How hiring works (short)

Referral first (employee referral bonuses are common), then recruiter outreach on LinkedIn, then applications via company career pages (many run on Comeet or Greenhouse/Lever) and boards. Detailed loops are in `cv-and-interviews.md`.

## Engineering management path

- Titles: Team Lead (hands-on, 3-8 people), R&D Group/Team Manager, Director of R&D/Engineering, VP R&D (usually top engineering role at a startup or scale-up), CTO (often founder-level). "R&D Manager" often means a manager of developers.
- Expectation: managers at Israeli startups are frequently still technical and may code or review designs; pure people-manager roles are more common at multinationals and larger companies.
- What employers screen for: Hebrew often matters more for management because teams are Hebrew-speaking (ask early which language the team uses in standups and design reviews), local network and references, delivery track record, hiring ability, and experience managing distributed teams.
- Managing from abroad: roles leading Israeli teams generally expect Israel-based leaders. For a US-based manager moving to Israel, global companies with Israel R&D sites may value a US-Israel bridge person; startups with US HQ and Israel R&D sometimes want a "bridge" VP or director.
- Compensation: see `compensation-and-benefits.md`. Managers' pay depends strongly on stage, equity, and scope; use multiple sources.
- Tip for a US manager: map US titles to Israeli ones, quantify team size, org scope, and outcomes, and note experience with distributed teams across time zones.
