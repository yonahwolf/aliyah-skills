# Working Remotely From Israel for a US Employer (and the Reverse)

Last verified: November 2026.

IMPORTANT: This file is general orientation, NOT tax or legal advice. Cross-border employment and tax interact in ways that depend on citizenship, residency dates, employer structure, and contract terms. Before moving, signing, or changing payroll, consult a cross-border accountant who handles both Israeli and US tax, and an Israeli employment lawyer for contracts. Several points below come from vendor (employer-of-record) marketing pages and are flagged; verify them against official sources.

## Decision tree

1. Does the US employer agree to employ someone living in Israel? (HR, legal, tax, security, and customer-contract constraints.)
2. How will they employ you in Israel?
   - Employer of record (EOR) in Israel: a local entity employs you on paper, handles Israeli payroll, pension, and social insurance, and invoices your US employer. Most common and lowest friction for the employer.
   - Employer's own Israeli subsidiary/branch: best for scale; the employer must set up and run Israeli payroll.
   - Independent contractor: you invoice as an Israeli self-employed person (osek murshe or osek patur). Highest risk of misclassification and permanent-establishment issues.
   - Keep US payroll (employee of the US company, living in Israel): often not feasible without registration; creates Israeli tax and social-insurance obligations and permanent-establishment risk for the employer.
3. Your tax residency timeline and benefits (below).
4. US citizen obligations (below).
5. Social insurance, health, pension implications.
6. Time zone and schedule fit.
7. The pitch to the employer.

## Employee vs contractor vs EOR

- Israeli courts decide employee vs contractor on the economic reality using a multi-factor test (control, integration, exclusivity, payment method, tools, economic risk, duration, intent); the contract label does not decide. If reclassified, the company may owe retroactive severance, pension, social insurance, leave, and recuperation pay. (A vendor page describes a 9-factor test and estimates; treat details as UNVERIFIED and ask a lawyer.)
- Long-term, exclusive, full-time work for a single foreign client looks like employment; a contractor structure there is risky for both sides.
- EOR providers that advertise Israel coverage include Deel, Remote, Oyster, Rivermate, Multiplier, and Horizons, plus Israel-based providers such as CWS Israel; verify each one covers Israel today. This is a list of examples, not endorsements. Compare fees, who bears the severance/pension (Section 14) and equity handling (Section 102 plans, RSUs), onboarding time (vendors claim as short as 24-48 hours once documents are in order; treat as marketing), termination procedures, and whether they support olim tax benefits.
- Olim-specific: a vendor markets an "Olim First Steps" EOR offering; Nefesh B'Nefesh does not itself employ you or run payroll. Do not describe NBN as an employer of record.
- Employees in Israel are entitled to statutory benefits (pension, severance, vacation, sick, recuperation, notice, reserve-duty protections). EOR employment includes these; make sure the total cost to the US employer is understood: Israeli employer costs on top of gross salary are significant (several tens of percent). Use an employer cost calculator from the EOR vendor and treat it as an estimate.

## Israeli tax: what applies to a new oleh working remotely

Facts from the Israel Tax Authority's new-immigrants guide (Oct 2026) and the Innovation Authority/Deloitte summaries:

- 10-year exemption: new olim and long-term returning residents (abroad 10+ years) are exempt from Israeli tax on qualifying foreign-source income and assets for 10 years (returning residents abroad about six years: 5 years, non-business income). For people becoming residents on or after 1 Jan 2026, income and assets under the exemption must be reported even if exempt; those who arrived before that date are exempt from reporting under the older rules.
- Source rules matter: the foreign-income exemption is for foreign-source income. Salary for work performed physically in Israel is generally treated as Israeli-source regardless of where the employer is, so you should NOT assume the 10-year exemption covers a US salary earned while working from Israel. Confirm with a cross-border accountant and the Tax Authority.
- New temporary benefit (2026 law): olim and returning residents who become Israeli residents between 5 Nov 2025 and 31 Dec 2026 (as stated by the Innovation Authority and Deloitte; the Tax Authority guide mentions aliyah between 5 Nov 2025 and 10 Feb 2027, a discrepancy to verify) may be exempt on Israeli-source employment, business, or professional income up to annual caps: NIS 600,000 in 2026, NIS 1 million in 2027 and 2028, NIS 350,000 in 2029, NIS 150,000 in 2030; lower caps for income from relatives. Conditions include genuinely moving the center of life, 75 days in Israel in each of 2027 and 2028 and in other specified years, and not postponing residency via a "year of adjustment." Passive income is excluded. This could apply to remote employment income earned in Israel; the interplay with an EOR or foreign employer needs advisor confirmation.
- Tax credit points for olim: extra credit points for the first 54 months in months 1-12 (1 point), 13-30 (3 points), 31-42 (2 points), and 43-54 (1 point). A point is reported as NIS 2,904/year for 2026.
- Residency: Israeli residence turns on "center of life"; presumptions include 183 days or more in Israel in a year, or 30 days or more in the year and 425 days over the current and two prior years.
- Standard brackets reach 47% (plus surtax at high incomes); see `compensation-and-benefits.md`.
- Reporting: file Israeli annual returns if required; keep records of foreign accounts and assets for arrivals from 1 Jan 2026.
- Get a pre-aliyah consultation: the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration and the Israel Tax Authority have "request for consultation" services (Tax Authority information center: *4954).

## US citizens: obligations (general rules; verify)

- US citizens remain subject to US tax on worldwide income and must file US returns while living in Israel. Israeli tax residency does not end US filing.
- Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE, Form 2555): excludes up to USD 132,900 of foreign earned income for 2026 (IRS Notice 2026-25), if you meet the bona fide residence or physical presence test. The housing exclusion has separate limits. FEIE does not reduce self-employment tax.
- Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116): credits Israeli income tax paid against US tax on the same income; often more useful than FEIE for high Israeli tax. Choice depends on income level and structure; the accountant should model both.
- US-Israel income tax treaty: exists and covers income tax (not social security); it contains a saving clause for US citizens. Ask your accountant how it applies.
- Social security (important): per the Social Security Administration's list, Israel is NOT among the countries with a US totalization agreement (checked Nov 2026; Romania listed with an effective date of 1 Sep 2026). Without totalization, there is risk of double contributions: US FICA/SECA (US citizens working for an American employer abroad are generally still covered by FICA; self-employed US citizens owe SECA regardless of residence) and Israeli Bituach Leumi. A vendor article claims a 2026 Knesset amendment (Amendment 262) exempts eligible new US immigrants from Israeli National Insurance for five years; this is UNVERIFIED, so check the Bituach Leumi site before relying on it. Ask the accountant to determine which contributions apply for your structure.
- Information reporting: FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) for foreign financial accounts above the aggregate threshold (USD 10,000), and FATCA Form 8938 above higher thresholds for people abroad; Israeli pension funds, keren hishtalmut, and certain Israeli investment products may have special or adverse US tax treatment (for example PFIC or foreign trust rules); get professional advice before choosing savings vehicles.
- Estimated payments and state taxes: some US states pursue former residents; check domicile rules.
- Equity: Israeli Section 102 treatment does not map automatically to US tax; model both.
- Health: US coverage may lapse; Israeli health insurance is via a kupat cholim.

## Bituach Leumi, health, pension, and savings

- Olim are insured via Bituach Leumi; health coverage is through a health fund (kupat cholim) that you must join. Employees have health tax and National Insurance deducted through payroll. Contribution rates depend on income; see the Bituach Leumi rate table.
- Pension: mandatory employer pension contributions apply after a qualifying period; ask the EOR about pension selection and fees.
- Keren hishtalmut: optional but often provided; tax-advantaged up to a ceiling; withdrawal rules favor a six-year hold. For US citizens, evaluate US tax impact.
- Severance and Section 14 terms: confirm in the EOR contract.
- Reserve duty: employers must honor reserve duty; Bituach Leumi reimburses salary.
- Keep documentation for future benefit claims (unemployment, maternity), which depend on contribution history.

## Permanent establishment (PE) and employer risk

- A US company with an employee working from Israel risks creating an Israeli taxable presence (PE) that can trigger Israeli corporate tax and payroll compliance. OECD-model updates (2025) say a home office generally does not create a PE if the employee works less than about 50% of the time in that jurisdiction or there is no commercial reason for the work to be performed there; Israel generally follows but reportedly takes a stricter view in some cases, such as key employees working from Israel or client-facing work. (Taxand/Herzog Fox & Neeman summary, Dec 2025.)
- Sales, contract-negotiating, senior executives, and exclusive long-term contractors raise PE risk. An EOR does not by itself eliminate PE risk but can help; legal review is required.
- Other employer issues: Israeli payroll registration, withholding, labor-law obligations, data protection, export controls, and customer security requirements.

## Time zone and workweek

- Israel is UTC+2 (UTC+3 in summer); US East is UTC-5 (UTC-4). The difference is usually 7 hours, but differing daylight-saving dates create a few weeks a year with 6 or 8 hours. US West is 10 hours behind usually. Sunday-Thursday is the Israeli workweek, with Friday often a short or off day and Shabbat on Saturday. Working US hours means evening work from about 4 pm Israel time onward; consider overlap windows (e.g., 3-4 hours) and async-first processes.
- Jewish holidays and Israeli school-year schedules affect availability; negotiate holidays and US holiday coverage in the contract.
- Burnout risk is high with late meetings plus morning family responsibilities; propose clear core hours.
- Security and connectivity: keep a backup internet, power plans, and a shelter protocol; employers sometimes ask about emergency coverage.

## Pitching the employer

1. Lead with business case: retention of a proven employee; timezone coverage for EMEA/APAC; no relocation cost; the employee remains the same cost center.
2. Offer to bear complexity: propose an EOR with transparent fees, a fixed contract, an accountant, and a compliance checklist; propose a trial period (for example 3-6 months after the move).
3. Identify risk and mitigation: PE analysis, data security, equipment, travel to US HQ quarterly, core hours.
4. Be flexible on compensation (local pay scales, benefits cost, currency).
5. Use a precise ask and timeline, and give the employer legal-friendly documentation.

Example message: "I'm planning to move to Israel in [month]. I'd like to continue in my role. I've researched employer-of-record options (e.g., X, Y) which would handle Israeli payroll and compliance with no entity needed. Could we schedule 30 minutes with HR/legal to review? I can propose core hours of [time] ET and cover key meetings."

## Working for an Israeli employer from the US (brief)

- If you live in the US and work for an Israeli company: the company may use a US employer-of-record or a US subsidiary for payroll (state payroll tax, benefits, I-9), or treat you as a contractor (1099); misclassification risk exists in both countries.
- Tax: you are a US tax resident and pay US federal and state tax on wages; the Israeli employer should not withhold Israeli tax on work performed in the US (confirm). If you become an Israeli resident later, the rules change.
- Equity: Israeli option plans for US taxpayers (Section 102 with US tax overlay) require advice.
- Pay in NIS or USD: consider currency risk (the shekel's strength in 2026 has pressured Israeli employers).
- Benefits: US health insurance usually comes through the EOR/US entity.
- If you plan to make aliyah later, a US-employer-through-Israel EOR or an Israeli employer is a smoother path.
- Always use a cross-border accountant.

## Questions to ask your accountant (copy list)

1. Given my aliyah date, am I eligible for the 2026 Israeli-source income exemption? What are the exact conditions and reporting?
2. Is my remote salary Israeli-source? How does the 10-year foreign exemption apply to me?
3. How should my US salary be structured (EOR vs contractor vs subsidiary) for Israeli and US tax and social insurance?
4. Do I pay both Bituach Leumi and FICA/SECA? Is there any exemption I can use?
5. FEIE vs Foreign Tax Credit: which works better for me for the next five years?
6. Which Israeli pension/hishtalmut/investment vehicles are safe from adverse US tax treatment?
7. What FBAR/FATCA reporting do I need? Do I need a Tax Authority pre-ruling?
8. What happens if I leave Israel before 2028 or 2029 (clawback of the new benefit)?
