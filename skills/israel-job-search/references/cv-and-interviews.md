# CV and Interviews in Israel

Last verified: November 2026. Conventions below combine press/blog guidance and general practice; they are norms, not rules. Older blog posts cited here are dated and flagged.

## CV conventions

- Length: one page is the norm for most professionals; two pages for senior people (management, long careers, academics may use more).
- Format: clean PDF (not Word) in English; add a Hebrew version for Hebrew-speaking employers and the public sector. Applicant tracking systems (including Comeet-based pipelines) parse plain structured text better than graphics.
- Photo: tech employers generally do not expect a photo, and many recommend omitting it; non-tech Hebrew-market CVs sometimes still include one. Do not include date of birth, marital status, or ID number; Israeli equalities law discourages employers from asking for irrelevant personal data.
- Military service: Israeli CVs routinely list army/national service (unit, role, years, achievements, skills), often near the education section. Employers treat it as a proxy for maturity, responsibility, and sometimes technical depth. For non-veterans, do not leave an unexplained gap: state aliyah date and what you did (study, work, volunteering) and let experience and projects carry the weight. Never imply service you did not do.
- Education: degree, field, institution, years, honors. Add whether your degree has been recognized (see `olim-programs-and-credentials.md`) if the employer or sector requires it.
- Skills: group by category (languages, frameworks, cloud, data, tools); no star ratings.
- Experience: reverse chronological; 3-5 bullets per role with measurable impact (scale, latency, cost, revenue, team size, scope). For managers: team size, org scope, hiring, delivery results, budget.
- Language line: state Hebrew honestly (none / basic / conversational / fluent). Misrepresenting Hebrew is quickly exposed in interviews.
- Links: LinkedIn, GitHub, portfolio, talks, open source. For juniors this section is critical.
- Cover letters: short (3-5 sentences) in the email body, mention referral, why the company, and aliyah timing/work authorization. Some employers ignore them.
- Work authorization: olim have the right to work immediately upon aliyah; pre-aliyah candidates should state arrival date and the intent to relocate. Foreign (non-Jewish, non-oleh) candidates need a work visa, which is a different path.
- References: have 2-3 ready; Israeli employers often check them late in the process. Brief your referees (role, key points).

## Interview loops by company type

Typical (not guaranteed) sequences. Ask recruiters to describe the loop up front.

| Stage | Startup | Multinational | Defense-adjacent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen | Recruiter or founder call (15-30 min) | Recruiter screen, sometimes online assessment | Recruiter plus eligibility check (citizenship, clearance, service history) |
| Technical | Home assignment (often 24-72 hours) and/or live coding and system design | Coding rounds (data structures/algorithms), system design, domain deep-dive | Domain-heavy (embedded, algorithms, C++/Python), longer timelines |
| Team/manager | Hiring manager, team members, sometimes whole team | Panel loop, behavioral interviews, leveling | Manager plus background interviews |
| Executive | CTO/CEO interview common | VP/Director for senior roles | Senior management plus security review |
| Close | References, offer, negotiation | HR/compensation committee, offer | Clearance process can take months |

Notes:
- Overall timeline: commonly 3-8 weeks; recent market reports describe hiring cycles lengthening to roughly 45-60 days.
- A 2021 Times of Israel guide lists typical steps: CV screen, short recruiter call, hiring-manager interview, home assignment, sometimes a presentation, reference checks, CEO interview at small firms, negotiation; total about 1-3 months. A 2013 oleh account says algorithm and data-structure rigor often exceeds US interviews. Both posts are dated; use them as flavor, not facts.
- Preparation: algorithms and data structures (LeetCode-style), system design (scalability, caching, queues, database tradeoffs), your own past projects in depth, plus behavioral stories (conflict, failure, leadership).
- Management candidates: expect questions on hiring and firing, performance management, roadmap trade-offs, working with product and with executives, and cross-site collaboration; there may be a peer manager round and a case study.
- Take-home assignments: ask the time budget; push back if it exceeds a few hours; do not do unpaid work that looks like production work for the employer.

## Hebrew vs English

- Most tech R&D teams in Israel can work in English because documentation, code, and customers are in English; some teams hold meetings in Hebrew, which is a practical disadvantage for non-speakers (social and promotion dynamics included).
- Roles needing Hebrew: government and public sector, defense and security roles, banks, telecom, healthcare, education, law, accounting, local sales/support, HR, anything customer-facing in Israel.
- Roles that work in English: global product companies, international sales and marketing roles, English-language support, remote roles, startups with US HQ and an English-speaking leadership team.
- Plan: start Hebrew pre-aliyah; use the Ministry of Aliyah ulpan (free for a period after aliyah; verify current entitlement and hours); consider evening ulpan or tutors; aim for conversational Hebrew within the first year if you want long-term career growth beyond English-first niches.
- Ask in screens: "What language do the team and stand-ups use?" "Is Hebrew required for promotion or management?"

## Culture and style in interviews

- Directness is expected; tone is more informal than the US; interviewers may ask about army service, family plans, and salary expectations early (some of these questions are unlawful if discriminatory, but you will hear them).
- If asked about army service as a non-veteran: answer briefly and positively, e.g., "I made aliyah at [age] and didn't serve; my work/projects show X; I'm committed to the country and open to [reserve or volunteer service if true]." Do not apologize repeatedly.
- Salary: expect to be asked for expectations in the first call. Give a researched range (see `compensation-and-benefits.md`), state whether it is gross monthly NIS, and ask what "gross" includes.
- Hard negotiation is normal but be respectful; Israelis expect some back-and-forth.

## Negotiation items (Israeli context)

Base monthly gross, whether it is "global" (all-in) or standard (with separately listed pension/severance), annual bonus, equity (ESOP/RSU and refresh), keren hishtalmut, car or car allowance, meals/Cibus/10bis card, phone, recuperation pay (dmei havraah), vacation days beyond the legal minimum, work from home days, reserve-duty policy, notice period, relocation help, and signing bonus. The contract may be in Hebrew; have it reviewed by a labor lawyer or a Hebrew-reading friend.
