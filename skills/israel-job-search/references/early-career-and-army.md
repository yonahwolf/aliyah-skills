# Early Career, Army Service, and First Jobs

Last verified: November 2026. IDF and Ministry rules change; the age/service table below comes from an undated Nefesh B'Nefesh PDF and a 2016 news report, so confirm with the IDF olim unit, NBN, or the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration before the user acts. This is not legal advice.

## The market for new graduates

- Entry-level tech is the hardest part of the market right now. A mid-2025 snapshot found only about 1.5% of tech vacancies open to people with no experience and employers asking for roughly three years even for "junior" roles; AI tools have replaced some junior training pipelines. Ynet (2026) reports junior demand still declining and manual QA eroding. (Globes, 11 Jun 2025; Ynet 2026)
- Over 83% of openings were in the Tel Aviv/central region in the 2025 snapshot, and most openings are engineering roles.
- Relatively better for juniors: roles with data/AI/cloud/security/embedded skills, internships and student positions that convert, and large multinationals with structured graduate or intern programs. Verify each program's current status and eligibility on the company career page.
- Practical timeline: plan for a 6-9 month search with a financial runway and a fallback (support engineer, QA automation, implementation/solutions engineer, data analyst, IT/DevOps junior, technical writer).

## How army service affects hiring

- In Israeli tech, service (especially in technology, intelligence, cyber, and elite units, and also in combat roles for trust/leadership signal) works as a trusted filter and an informal network. Startup founders and many hiring managers are veterans. Alumni networks refer each other.
- For non-veterans (including most olim who arrive after the mandatory age) this means:
  - Fewer automatic introductions and weaker "signal" on the CV
  - A real need to replace the signal: strong internships, competitions, projects/open source, research, references from known engineers, and a referral
  - Occasional questions in interviews about "why no service" (answer briefly and positively)
- It is not a bar everywhere: many multinationals, startups with US founders, and English-language roles hire non-veterans routinely, and employers are generally used to olim who did not serve.
- Do not claim service you did not do, and do not suggest service solely as a hiring tactic: it is a significant personal commitment with its own risks.

## Rules for olim and IDF service (verify)

From NBN's "Aliyah and the IDF" PDF (undated) for single olim:
- Age 18-19 at aliyah: men typically 32 months, women 24 months
- Age 20-21: 24 months (men and women)
- Age 22-27: no mandatory service; may volunteer (non-combat about 18 months, combat about 24 months), subject to IDF review
- Age 28 and older: generally exempt and not eligible for the regular volunteer track
- Married people and medical professionals follow different rules
- A 2016 Times of Israel report states the IDF stopped drafting new immigrants aged 22-26. The NBN table is consistent with that.

Other routes (descriptions only; confirm terms):
- Mahal (Mitnadvei Chutz LaAretz): a volunteer track for non-Israeli (non-oleh) Jews from abroad who are not planning to stay; short service. This is for temporary volunteers, not olim; olim usually use other routes. Verify current eligibility.
- Garin Tzabar: a program for young adults from abroad (including future olim) who serve as lone soldiers; participants serve two years and are not affected by the age rules mentioned above per the 2016 report. Check the current programs and application cycles.
- Lone soldier (chayal boded): a service status for soldiers without family in Israel, with benefits administered via the IDF and organizations (the Association for the Wellbeing of Israel's Soldiers, Lone Soldier Center, others); verify support and the current benefits.
- "Olim Al Bet" (reported by JNS, Jan 2026): an IDF/Ministry of Aliyah and Integration initiative reported to offer olim aged 26 and older a condensed two-week basic training track to serve as reservists (full eligibility details were not retrievable). Verify details and status.
- Reserve duty: after service, veterans serve in reserves; reserve duty obligations can affect work schedules; employers' attitudes vary but are accommodating in general.
- National service (Sherut Leumi) and civilian volunteer year programs exist for women and others; check the Ministry and organizations.

## How non-veteran olim position themselves

1. Lead with demonstrated skills: projects, internships, competitions (hackathons, ICPC-style contests), open source, published research, certifications that are job-relevant.
2. Show Israel commitment: aliyah date, Hebrew progress, volunteering, living in the periphery if relevant, optional reserve or volunteer service only if genuinely intended.
3. Get referrals: from university or alumni networks, internships, olim engineers, employers who hire many olim, NBN contacts.
4. Target employers that value English: global companies, US-HQ startups, products for US/EU customers, sales engineering and customer-facing technical roles, English-language support.
5. Use the bridge: an internship or student job at a US company, or remote work (see `remote-for-us-employers.md`), builds experience while the Israeli network grows.
6. Treat Hebrew as an investment that opens doors for later growth, not a prerequisite for every junior role.

## Degrees recognized in Israel

Overseas degrees from recognized institutions are generally recognized through the Ministry's degree evaluation for olim (see `olim-programs-and-credentials.md`). For private tech employers the degree matters less than skills; for public sector, teaching, and regulated fields it is required. Check admission rules at the university if the user wants to study further (Israeli master's programs; some universities run English-language programs for olim and internationals; Braude and Reichman have olim-focused programs per their sites; verify).

## First-job strategy

- Internships and student positions (during or after university): apply broadly; many firms recruit interns six to twelve months ahead. Student roles can convert to full-time.
- Graduate programs at large companies: check Intel, Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Apple, banks, and defense companies; ask for English-language process.
- Government and defense-adjacent: often require Israeli citizenship (olim become citizens at aliyah), clearance, and may have service filters; ask.
- Startups: referrals and a sharp project beat paper credentials; small teams may hire a junior who can do the work.
- Career centers at Israeli universities: even post-graduation, ask about alumni access.
- Support organizations: NBN employment team, NBN Upgrade courses (including a "Startup Career Israel" track for young professionals and students planning to move), alumni networks, young olim communities.
