# Neighborhood Guides

Last verified: November 2026 for structure and sources. The profiles are qualitative, drawn from common community knowledge and general reporting, not from official data. Check the details yourself.

## Read this first

- Prices and rents change monthly and vary block to block. This file gives relative tiers (lower, mid, high, very high compared with other Israeli areas) and no exact figures. Do not quote prices from it.
- How to check current numbers:
  - Nadlan.gov.il (official sale transactions with addresses, by street).
  - Madlan (madlan.co.il) and Yad2 (yad2.co.il): live listings, asking prices, price history. Asking prices are higher than real deals.
  - Central Bureau of Statistics (cbs.gov.il): housing price index by district, published every two months.
  - Globes (en.globes.co.il), Calcalist (ctech or calcalistech.com), Times of Israel monthly housing snapshots: market reports in English.
  - Local Facebook and WhatsApp groups, and a local broker who works the street.
- Community type labels: religious (Orthodox, of various streams), secular, mixed. In many cities these shift by street. Visit on Shabbat and a weekday evening.
- Visit before you commit. Rent for a year first if possible.
- Commute: check actual travel with Moovit, Google Maps, or Rav-Kav, at the time of day you will travel. Train lines (Israel Railways) are fast on the coastal and Jerusalem lines; buses and light rail help. Check current line status and schedules, since lines and works change.
- Safe-room (mamad) and shelter access vary by building age; ask for it.
- Locations near disputed or contested areas: this file notes where a neighborhood or town lies beyond the 1949 armistice lines. Its legal status is contested internationally and treated differently under Israeli law and in international discussion. Practical differences can affect land registration, some banks' or insurers' practices, and travel advice. If that matters to you, research it yourself with a lawyer before committing; this guide takes no position.

## Jerusalem

General: hilly, dense, many Anglo olim, the largest English-speaking community in Israel for religious families. Light rail (the Red Line) and buses; train to Tel Aviv (the fast line to Ben Gurion and Tel Aviv HaHagana) from Yitzhak Navon station, check schedule. Prices generally high for what you get; renting is competitive in summer. Many apartments lack elevators or parking. Shabbat shuts public transport.

## Tel Aviv area (Gush Dan)

General: Tel Aviv is the most expensive major city per square meter; rents are high; the lifestyle is secular and fast. Surrounding cities are often better value with train access. The Tel Aviv light rail (Red Line) and buses connect; the train lines (Ayalon) connect Tel Aviv, Herzliya, Netanya, Beersheba, and Ben Gurion airport. Bnei Brak is a very religious, dense city; usually not for most English-speaking olim unless they seek a Haredi community.

## Herzliya, Herzliya Pituach

General: coastal, affluent, high-tech employers, good train access (Herzliya station on the coastal line), and the home of Reichman University. Herzliya Pituach is a very high-priced beachfront and business district; central Herzliya is high; Neve Amal and other inland neighborhoods are mid. Mixed secular and traditional, with some Anglo religious community in specific neighborhoods. A strong choice for students, tech workers, and young families.

## Raanana

General: the best-known Anglo-heavy city in the center, with a large English-speaking religious and mixed community, parks, schools, and a suburban feel. Price tier: high to very high. Commute: buses to Tel Aviv and Herzliya; train stations are on the edges of town or in neighboring cities, so check the walking or feeder-bus distance for the specific address. Many English-speaking services, synagogues, and community organizations.

## Kfar Saba, Hod Hasharon

General: adjoining suburbs to Raanana, somewhat lower in price tier than Raanana, with train stations on the Sharon line. Kfar Saba has a growing Anglo community and a mix of religious and secular; Hod Hasharon is a family-oriented city with newer neighborhoods. Price tier: mid to high.

## Modiin (Modiin-Maccabim-Reut)

General: planned city between Jerusalem and Tel Aviv, family oriented, with strong schools, parks, a train station (Modiin Center), and an active Anglo community. The city is well-planned, with many new buildings. Commute: train to Tel Aviv and Jerusalem (check the line). Price tier: mid to high. Mixed religious and secular. Both the main train station and Highway 1 matter for the commute.

## Beit Shemesh (including Ramat Beit Shemesh)

General: a large Anglo religious community, especially in Ramat Beit Shemesh (Aleph, Bet, and Gimmel areas), with relatively lower prices than Jerusalem or Raanana. The city's communities vary a lot; Haredi and Dati Leumi neighborhoods are adjacent, so check the street's character. Commute: train to Jerusalem and Tel Aviv from Beit Shemesh station (not close to all neighborhoods) and buses. Price tier: mid. Primarily religious.

## Netanya

General: coastal city with a large French-speaking and Anglo population, a beach, and train access (the coastal line). Prices are mid to high depending on the proximity to the sea. Mixed communities; the Anglo-religious community is concentrated in some neighborhoods. Commute to Tel Aviv by train is about 30 to 40 minutes (check schedule).

## Haifa

General: Israel's third city, on the northern coast and Mount Carmel, with a lower price tier than the center, the Technion and University of Haifa, a mixed Jewish and Arab population, and a quieter pace. Strong for students and for those who want more space and better value. Anglo community is smaller. Commute: train to Tel Aviv (about 1 hour), a subway-like Carmelit, and buses. Price tier: lower to mid. Mixed.

## Beersheba

General: the capital of the Negev, a growing tech and university hub (Ben-Gurion University, a cyber park), with significantly lower prices than the center, a good train link to Tel Aviv, and new development. Anglo community is small. Price tier: lower. Mixed. Relevant for those who want government incentives for periphery areas; priority-area rental assistance may apply (confirm).

## Gush Etzion and Efrat

General: a cluster of communities south of Jerusalem with a strong Anglo religious community, especially in Efrat, with good schools and a community feel. Located in the West Bank; the legal and diplomatic status of these areas is disputed. Practical differences can affect land registration, financing and insurance practices, government incentives, and travel advice; if relevant to you, research it yourself with a lawyer. Commute to Jerusalem by bus or car; check road and traffic conditions. Price tier: mid to high.

## Student-friendly areas near Reichman University (Herzliya)

- Reichman's campus is in Herzliya, with on-campus dorms (about 748 units) for Israeli and international students. Contact dorms@runi.ac.il for eligibility, cost, and deadlines. Reichman's own pages list off-campus housing platforms and groups.
- Off-campus: apartments in Herzliya (central Herzliya and the neighborhoods near the campus), shared apartments, and sublets. Check how far the campus is from the apartment by walking, bike, or bus. Herzliya Pituach is pricey; student groups often rent in central and inland Herzliya.
- Commute links:
  - Train: Herzliya station is on the coastal line, with direct trains to Tel Aviv stations (check for the closest to your destination) and north to Netanya and Haifa. Check schedule and the station's distance from your apartment and campus.
  - Bus: local Herzliya buses link the station and the campus; intercity buses to Tel Aviv and Raanana.
  - Bike: Herzliya is bike-friendly; shared e-bikes and scooters exist, though rules and operators change.
- Tel Aviv: students who live in Tel Aviv (such as Florentin, Ramat Aviv, or north Tel Aviv) commute by train or bus; check peak-time travel. Friends often share apartments in Tel Aviv and take the train to Herzliya.
- Cost: Herzliya and Tel Aviv are among the pricier markets for student rentals; cost per room in a shared apartment is lower than for a studio. Dorms are generally easier for a first year.
- Summer and holidays: check if the lease covers the whole year or only the academic year.
- Olim students: check whether living in student housing affects rental assistance, and ask the municipality about student arnona discounts.

## Other notes

- Anglo-friendly does not mean cheaper. Anglo-popular areas often carry a premium.
- Compare areas on the same criteria: monthly cost for a 3-room apartment, commute to work or school, the nearest shul or community, schools, supermarkets, transport on Shabbat, and noise.
- For families: schools and kindergartens (gan) matter; apply and confirm registration deadlines with the municipality.
- Periphery areas (Negev, Galilee) may carry incentives and lower prices. Check government incentive lists for current eligible communities.
