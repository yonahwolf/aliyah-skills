# Renting in Israel

Last verified: November 2026. Not legal advice. Have a lawyer review any lease you cannot fully read.

## How the market works

- Most residents rent in the private market from individual owners. Institutional or professionally managed rental is small, though the government has discussed expanding it (as of a Nov 2025 report, proposals were still in draft).
- Typical lease: one year, often with a tenant option for a second year. Rent is usually paid monthly by post-dated checks or bank transfer. Rent increases and linkage to the CPI are negotiated terms, not automatic.
- Rooms are counted Israeli-style: living room counts. A "3-room" apartment usually has two bedrooms and a living room.
- Peak season for turnover is summer (June to September), tied to the school year and university start. Expect more competition and faster decisions then. Good listings go within days.
- Rents are usually quoted per month in shekels and do not include arnona, vaad bayit, or utilities.
- Prices: do not rely on any figure from a blog. Check live listings (Madlan, Yad2), compare 10 or more comparable listings per area, and treat asking prices as the ceiling, not the market clearing price. Recent coverage (Oct 2026) reported falling sale prices while rent coverage was mixed; check current reports.

## Where to search

- Yad2 (yad2.co.il): largest listings site, Hebrew-first, mix of owners and brokers.
- Madlan (madlan.co.il): listings plus neighborhood data, price history, and transaction info.
- Facebook groups: city-specific Anglo groups (for example for Jerusalem, Modiin, Raanana, Beit Shemesh, Netanya, Tel Aviv, Herzliya) and general "apartments for rent" groups. Search by city name plus "apartments" or "olim". Join a few and read the rules.
- WhatsApp groups: neighborhood and community groups, often posted in synagogue or community networks. Ask people in the community.
- Janglo (janglo.net): English-language classifieds and housing for newcomers.
- Homeless (homeless.co.il) and Winwin (winwin.co.il): older Hebrew listing sites, still used.
- Brokers (metavchim): useful for speed and for small-print, especially for newcomers. See below.
- Word of mouth: shul, community, employer relocation help, the university housing office.
- For students: Reichman publishes lists of housing platforms and groups; see the Student housing section below.

## Brokers and brokerage fees (dmei tivuch)

- Common norm: a rental broker fee is about one month's rent plus VAT (VAT is 18 percent as of 2025 and 2026; check). Brokerage fees are not capped by law as far as sources reviewed show.
- Under the Fair Rental Law, if the landlord hired the broker, the landlord pays that broker in full. In practice, tenants often pay when they engaged the broker themselves or signed a brokerage agreement. Read any brokerage agreement before viewing: signing it can create an obligation to pay if you rent through that broker.
- Dual representation: ask who the broker works for. A broker for both sides must disclose it in writing (broker conduct rules in force since March 8, 2025, as reported in a September 2026 article; verify).
- Under those rules brokers may not draft contracts and may not charge advance fees unless the contract provides for them. Ads must show the broker's name and license number. Ask for the license number and verify it with the Ministry of Justice registry.
- Negotiate: fee amount, whether it is payable only on signing, and whether you can skip the broker for an owner-direct listing.
- Do not pay cash fees without a written agreement and a tax invoice.

## Typical lease (hozeh sechirut) terms

- Term: usually 12 months. An option to extend for another 12, with the rent for that year fixed or capped, is common and worth negotiating.
- Rent: monthly, in shekels. Check whether it is indexed to the CPI (madad); avoid open-ended indexation without a cap.
- Early exit: negotiate a clause allowing exit with notice (for example 60 or 90 days) against a defined penalty, or the right to find a replacement tenant.
- Subletting: usually requires landlord consent. Clarify before signing if roommates may change.
- Keys, condition, and inventory: attach a written list of furniture and appliances with photos.
- Landlord's right to enter: reasonable notice only, for repairs or showing the apartment near the end of the lease.
- Disputes: the contract may name a court; ask a lawyer.
- Registration: leases of up to five years are generally not registered at the land registry; ask your lawyer.

## Guarantees and deposits

The Fair Rental Law (Hok Sechirut Hogenet, in force from 17 September 2017) limits how much security a landlord may demand when it is held as a cash-like instrument (cash, post-dated checks, bank guarantee, deposit):

- Cap: the lower of one-third of the total rent for the whole lease or three monthly rents. Example from a 2017 summary: one-year lease at 5,000 NIS/month gives a cap of 15,000 NIS.
- Landlord may draw on it only for: unpaid rent, unpaid tenant-side bills (utilities, arnona, vaad bayit), damage beyond normal wear that the tenant did not repair, and failure to vacate.
- Return: sources say within 60 days after the lease ends. Confirm the exact statutory wording with a lawyer.
- The law separately allows other forms such as a promissory note (shtar chov) and guarantors. Rules on how these combine with the cap are technical; ask a lawyer whether the total security in your contract exceeds what the law allows. Legal limits generally apply to cash-like security; the interaction with notes and guarantors is a place where lawyers disagree, so do not assume.
- Tenants who are olim without Israeli credit history often face requests for a guarantor (aravim), a bank guarantee (aravut bankait), extra checks, or several months in advance. Negotiate: offer the legal maximum, a US-bank statement, an employer letter, or a parent's written guarantee. Landlords tend to be more flexible when you pay more up front or have a Nefesh B'Nefesh or employer reference.
- A bank guarantee requires an Israeli bank account and a fee. Ask the bank how long it takes.

## Post-dated checks and rent payment

- Common norm: tenant gives 12 post-dated checks (check "shekim") or a smaller number covering the lease period. Post-dated checks require a bank account and checkbook, which olim may not have for several weeks.
- Alternatives: monthly bank transfer or standing order (hora'at keva). Ask for these if your account is not ready.
- Before issuing checks, write the rent amount, dates, and recipient clearly, and keep a list.
- Never leave blank checks. A bounced check carries penalties and legal exposure in Israel.

## Tenant protections and landlord obligations (Fair Rental Law)

Summarized from law-firm and Globes summaries. Verify with a lawyer or the Knesset text:

- Applies to residential leases, with exceptions. Excluded categories reported include vacation rentals, student dormitories, protected tenancies, leases of three months or less or ten years or more, leases between relatives, and luxury rentals above about 20,000 NIS per month. Check whether yours is covered.
- Fit for habitation: the apartment must meet minimum standards such as running water, electricity, sewage, ventilation, lighting, and a lockable door. If it is not fit, the tenant may cancel.
- Repairs: ordinary repairs for faults arising from reasonable use: landlord must repair or pay within 30 days of a written demand. Urgent faults that prevent reasonable use (burst pipe, serious mold, no hot water or electrical failure) within 3 days. If the landlord fails, the tenant may repair and deduct the reasonable cost from rent, with proper notice and receipts. Keep all communications in writing (WhatsApp counts as a record but email or registered mail is better).
- Landlord pays building-improvement items and structural defects. Tenant pays everyday upkeep.
- Waivers: the law's tenant protections generally cannot be waived in the landlord's favor. A contract clause that reduces them may be void.
- The law does not set rent controls or cap increases. Increases are by contract.
- Enforcement is by court or small-claims route. A lawyer's letter often resolves disputes.

## Arnona, vaad bayit, utilities: who pays

- Arnona (municipal property tax): by default the tenant pays arnona for the period of occupancy, paid to the municipality. Transfer the arnona account into your name at the start of the lease. The landlord remains responsible in some cases, so read the lease.
- Olim arnona discount: customarily 50 to 90 percent depending on the municipality, up to 100 sqm, for one 12-month period used within two years of aliyah, requiring a lease of at least 12 months or proof of ownership. Water charges are not discounted. Confirm with your municipality. Students may also have discounts in some cities; check the municipal website.
- Vaad bayit (building committee fees): usually paid by the tenant for current maintenance, with special assessments for major repairs (such as roof or elevator replacement) often paid by the owner. Put it in the lease.
- Utilities (electricity, water, gas, internet, mobile): the tenant pays usage. See `utilities-and-setup.md`.

## Viewing checklist

Use the template in `SKILL.md`. Extra points for renters:
- Ask for the last 6 months of electricity and water bills.
- Ask why the current tenant is leaving.
- Ask about noise at night and weekends, nearby construction, and shelter access.
- Ask if the apartment has a mamad (safe room) or access to a miklat (shelter), because distance matters.
- Confirm the exact apartment number and the owner's identity.

## Roommates (shutafim)

- Common in cities among students and young professionals. Choose by routine and budget, and agree on cleaning, guests, shared costs, and what happens if someone leaves.
- Sign one lease with joint liability or separate agreements; a lawyer can advise. Joint liability means one roommate's missed rent can hit everyone. Ask the landlord about it.
- Put utilities in one name, with a shared payment method, and track splits in a simple app.
- Arnona discounts for olim in shared apartments are usually prorated to your share.

## Student housing

- Reichman University has on-campus dorms (about 748 units in three buildings, per the university page) housing Israeli and international students. Eligibility, cost, and deadlines are not listed on the page reviewed; contact the dorms team (dorms@runi.ac.il) and check the university housing page for the year.
- Off campus: the university lists platforms and Facebook groups for Herzliya and Tel Aviv rooms, sublets, and roommates (for example LiveHerz, Facebook groups for Herzliya and Tel Aviv housing, Janglo). See the RRIS housing page.
- Typical student arrangement: a shared apartment with a short or academic-year lease and a furnished room. Ask about summer coverage, since leases may run July to June or September to June.
- Other universities (Hebrew University, Tel Aviv University, Technion, Ben-Gurion, Bar-Ilan, and so on) have dorms and student-housing associations. Search the university's housing office page, the student union, and city-specific student Facebook groups.
- Students may qualify for municipal arnona reductions or for exemption from arnona in dorms. Ask the housing office and municipality.
- Olim students: check the olim rental assistance rules (they may overlap with student housing rules; living in subsidized housing can affect eligibility).

## Subletting

- Requires the landlord's written consent unless the lease permits it. Unauthorized subletting can end the lease.
- Short-term or Airbnb-type subletting is usually prohibited by lease or building rules.
- If you sublet, keep your own lease and arnona obligations clear, and use a written sublease.
- When taking a sublet, ask for proof that the subletter has the landlord's consent.

## Scams to avoid

- Request for advance payment or a deposit before you see the apartment, especially by wire or crypto.
- Listing with a price far below market, owner "abroad", and keys "by mail".
- Pressure to sign immediately or to hand over cash.
- A person who cannot show ownership documents or a verified authorization.
- Fake broker or fake rental agency; check the Ministry of Justice broker registry.
- Unrealistic photos or a duplicate listing under different names.
- Never transfer money to a stranger's account before a lease signing.

## Olim rental assistance and benefits

- Rental assistance from the Ministry of Construction and Housing: sources show the program was reformed in 2024. For aliyah on or after 1 March 2024, one source lists a basic monthly amount of about 363 NIS for a single oleh or oleh with a non-oleh spouse, 659 for a couple, 739 for a single parent, with higher supplements (973, 1,341, 1,500 NIS) in national priority areas, and payments starting around month 7. A Jerusalem Post report from June 2024 described the basic assistance as lasting about two years. Another NBN page says four years and an older table says up to five. These sources conflict. Confirm with Misrad HaBinui VeHaShikun (Ministry of Construction and Housing, *5442) or your local Misrad HaKlita.
- Disqualifiers reported: owning or having owned property in the 10 years before aliyah, staying in subsidized absorption housing, long trips abroad (a pause).
- Arnona discount: see above. Customs benefits: see `utilities-and-setup.md`. The arnona and customs benefits typically expire within two and three years of aliyah respectively.
- Many other benefits (sal klita, ulpan) are outside this skill.
