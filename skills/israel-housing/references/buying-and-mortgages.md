# Buying in Israel and Mortgages

Last verified: November 2026. Not legal, tax, or financial advice. Use an Israeli real estate lawyer (orech din mekarke'in) before signing and a licensed mortgage advisor (yoetz mashkanta) before committing.

## Market context (dated)

- Times of Israel, 30 Oct 2026 (citing the Central Bureau of Statistics and the Finance Ministry): CBS data for July-August 2026 showed average home prices down about 1.8 percent year over year and 0.6 percent against the prior two months; regional moves were mixed. August 2026 sales volumes were among the lowest for that month since 2000. New-apartment inventory was described as at a record. The Bank of Israel rate was held at 3.25 percent in late October 2026 after cuts earlier in the year. The article also cited a strong shekel (about 3.11 per dollar), which reduces the purchasing power of USD savings.
- Interpretation: do not read this as a forecast. Prices remain high versus incomes, and conditions vary by region and by new vs resale. Check the latest CBS release and Bank of Israel statements before deciding.
- Exchange rates matter for olim who convert USD to shekels. Plan the timing of conversions with the bank or a currency specialist, and keep documentation of where funds came from.

## Rent vs buy decision framework

Ask the user to compare, in shekels:
1. Total cost of owning for the period they expect to stay: down payment opportunity cost, mortgage interest, purchase tax, lawyer and broker, maintenance, vaad bayit, insurance, and later selling costs.
2. Rent saved, with a realistic rent growth range.
3. Their horizon: under 5 years usually favors renting; 7+ years can favor buying, but it depends on the market and on being able to keep the apartment.
4. Liquidity and risk: how much emergency cash remains after the down payment; income stability; currency exposure of savings.
5. Olim benefits tied to a time window: reduced purchase tax within seven years (and up to one year before aliyah), and the Zakaut mortgage within 15 years of oleh status. These are reasons to plan, not reasons to rush.
6. Non-financial factors: neighborhood fit, community, schools, ability to renovate.
7. Rule of thumb for stress testing: model mortgage payments at higher interest rates and with CPI-linked tracks rising.

Be balanced. Common mistakes: buying too soon in an unfamiliar area; assuming prices only rise; underestimating closing costs.

## Step-by-step purchase process (resale)

1. Budget: down payment, closing costs (below), reserve. Gather documents of income and savings (US tax returns, pay stubs, bank statements). Banks must verify the source of funds.
2. Mortgage approval in principle (ishur ekroni) from the bank and an independent licensed advisor. It tells you what you can borrow before you negotiate. Do this before searching seriously.
3. Pick a lawyer early. Tell them your budget and timeline. Lawyer fee is typically about 0.5 to 1.5 percent of the price plus VAT (a 2025-26 source; negotiable).
4. Search: Yad2, Madlan, brokers, Facebook groups, word of mouth. Check actual recent sale prices on Nadlan.gov.il (Ministry of Justice and Tax Authority transaction data) for comparables.
5. Inspect: independent engineer (mehandes bodek) or architect before committing. Check building condition, leaks, electrical, plumbing, mamad (safe room), building permit compliance, and unauthorized additions.
6. Negotiate in principle. Do not sign any memorandum or pay a deposit before a lawyer reviews. Under broker conduct rules in force since March 2025, brokers should not draft contracts; use your lawyer for legal documents.
7. Lawyer checks (the core of the process):
   - Land registry extract (nesach tabu) from the Israel Land Registry, to confirm owner, liens, mortgages, warnings (he'arot azhara), and the type of rights.
   - Rights (zchuyot): registered ownership or long lease in the Tabu (Land Registry); a lease from the Israel Land Authority (Rashut Mekarke'ei Yisrael, formerly the Minhal), where the lease may need to be capitalized or transferred (hivun, which is capitalization, and dmei heskem, consent fees); or rights registered only with a housing company (chevra meshakenet) or a developer, which are weaker until formally registered at the Tabu. Ask the lawyer to explain exactly what you are buying and what steps remain.
   - Municipal confirmation: arnona and water debts, building permits, Tofes 4 (certificate of occupancy), betterment levies (heitel hashbacha) and unpaid taxes of the seller.
   - Planning: nearby development plans, Tama (national plan) projects such as urban renewal (Pinui Binui, TAMA 38) status.
   - Seller identity, Power of Attorney, tax status of the seller.
8. Contract: your lawyer negotiates. The first payment (often a meaningful percentage of the price) is paid on signing; subsequent payments are scheduled by milestone. Typical percentages vary by deal, so verify with your lawyer rather than assume. Include: handover date, items included, who pays arnona and utilities until handover, penalties for delay, and guarantees.
9. Tax declaration: the buyer reports the purchase to the Israel Tax Authority and pays purchase tax (mas rechisha) within 60 days of signing, per a 2025-26 source; confirm the deadline for your case. Olim apply for the reduced rate (see below) with a lawyer.
10. Mortgage: after signing, the bank approves finally, orders an appraisal (shamai), and you sign mortgage documents. Many banks require life insurance and structure insurance. The loan is registered as a lien (mashkanta) on the property.
11. Payments and handover: balance payments, keys, final meter readings, and transfer of utilities and arnona.
12. Registration: the lawyer registers the buyer's rights at the Tabu or with the developer's or housing company's records. Keep copies of everything.

## New-build and "on paper" (al hanyar)

- Buying from a contractor before the building exists is common. Prices may include index linkage to the construction input index (madad tchulat habniya), which can raise the price over time.
- Protections: the Sale Law (Apartments) (Guarantee of Investments of Buyers) requires the contractor to give the buyer a guarantee or insurance for payments made. Ensure your lawyer verifies it; ask whether payments are protected by a bank guarantee (aravut hok mechira) or by pledge on the land.
- Risks: construction delays, contractor insolvency, quality of finish, unexpected index increases, and changes to plans. Check the developer's record and the permit stage.
- Typical process: reservation, contract (the developer's lawyer's draft; negotiate through your own lawyer), staged payments tied to construction milestones, handover and warranty periods (bedek bayit), and a final inspection.
- Developer's lawyer fee for new construction is capped for the buyer's share at a modest amount (a source cites up to 0.5 percent or about 5,770 NIS plus VAT, whichever is lower; verify the current cap).
- Government-subsidized lotteries (below) have their own rules.

## Purchase tax (mas rechisha) - dated figures

Source: Nefesh B'Nefesh article last updated 15 April 2026 and a second reputable guide; the gov.il calculator could not be fetched during research, so confirm on the Israel Tax Authority site. Thresholds are indexed to the CPI each January, so these 2026 thresholds are expected to change in January 2027.

Single residence (an Israeli resident's only residential property), 2026 thresholds:

| Portion of price (NIS) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to about 1,978,745 | 0% |
| About 1,978,745 to 2,347,040 | 3.5% |
| About 2,347,040 to 6,055,070 | 5% |
| About 6,055,070 to 20,183,565 | 8% |
| Above about 20,183,565 | 10% |

Additional residence: reported as 8 percent up to about 6,055,070 and 10 percent above. Foreign residents are reported to pay the same rates as an additional-residence buyer.

Olim reduced rates (for a sole residence, agreements signed on or after 15 August 2024), per the same sources:

| Portion of price (NIS) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to about 1,978,745 | 0% |
| About 1,978,745 to 6,055,070 | 0.5% |
| About 6,055,070 to 20,183,565 | 8% |
| Above 20,183,565 | Not covered by the olim track |

Time window: the contract must be signed up to one year before aliyah or within seven years after it. Army service time within the seven years was reported as not counted. New-construction contracts have a special rule: the construction period is not counted if aliyah occurs within three years of signing. Earlier olim (before 15 August 2024) may be able to choose between old and new rules. The older rate structure reported: 0.5 percent up to about 1,988,090, then 5 percent. Olim apply to the Tax Authority (a form for the immigrant partial exemption exists on gov.il) and the lawyer usually submits it.

Eligibility details (the definition of "single residence", the effect of owning another home, and whether you must sell an existing home within a period) are complicated. Have the lawyer check before signing.

## Capital gains tax on sale (mas shevach) - basics

- The seller pays mas shevach (real estate appreciation tax) on the gain. For a "single residence" meeting conditions (for example held for at least 18 months, no more than one such exemption in a set number of years), an exemption or reduced rate can apply, up to a ceiling on the sale price. Other sellers can pay up to about 25 percent of the real gain, with linear and other computation methods. These details change and have conditions, so confirm with a tax advisor or lawyer.
- Why it matters for buyers: it affects your future resale planning and rent vs buy math. If you are buying an additional property or are a non-resident, rules differ and are less favorable.
- Reporting and payment are due soon after the sale; the seller's lawyer handles it.

## Mortgages (mashkanta)

### Rules and limits (Bank of Israel)

- Loan-to-value (LTV) limits on housing loans, long-standing Bank of Israel rules: up to 75 percent for a first home buyer, 70 percent for an upgrader (a buyer replacing an existing apartment), and 50 percent for an investor or additional home. A non-resident or foreign buyer is generally treated as an investor (50 percent). Olim who are Israeli citizens or entitled to citizenship are reported to qualify as first-home buyers (up to 75 percent) on a mortgage page from a major bank; confirm your category with the bank.
- A February 2026 Globes report on new Bank of Israel supervisory instructions: repayment-to-income may not exceed 50 percent of disposable income (above 40 percent banks must use a higher risk weight, which tends to make lending stricter); variable-rate tracks (prime or CPI-linked variable) are limited to two-thirds of the mortgage so at least one-third must be fixed; a maximum term of 30 years; and loans against an existing property for any purpose are limited to 70 percent LTV.
- In practice, banks often use about 40 percent of net household income as their working repayment ceiling. Ask the bank.
- Equity: for most buyers, expect to bring 25 to 30 percent or more of the price in cash, plus purchase tax and fees.

### The multi-track structure (tamhil)

Israeli mortgages combine several "tracks" (maslulim) into one loan. Typical options:
- Prime-linked (Prime): floats with the Bank of Israel rate plus or minus a bank margin. Popular. Payment changes when the rate changes.
- Fixed, not CPI-linked (kvua lo tzmuda): stable payments, higher starting rate.
- Fixed, CPI-linked (kvua tzmuda): lower starting rate but the principal rises with inflation.
- Variable, not CPI-linked (mishtana): rate resets every few years.
- Variable, CPI-linked: more complex and riskier.
- The rule limiting variable tracks to two-thirds of the loan means at least one-third fixed.
- Compare the full structure on a simulator. Banks' offers differ by margin, track mix, and fees. Use two or three banks and an independent advisor to negotiate.
- Prepayment: many tracks allow early repayment with a fee for some types; ask the bank. The Zakaut mortgage reportedly carries no early repayment penalty.
- Stress test the structure: model a rate increase and inflation and check you could pay.
- Banks compete on rate margins; the Bank of Israel publishes mortgage rate averages and comparison tools.

### Mortgages for olim and US income

- The bank needs proof of income and of the source of the down payment. US documents commonly used: employment letter, pay stubs, two years of US tax returns, bank and brokerage statements, and a US credit report. Banks may want translations or apostilled documents. Ask early.
- Income earned abroad (including remote work for a US employer) is considered by some banks but can be weighted conservatively or require longer history. Self-employed borrowers face more documentation.
- Wire the down payment from abroad before signing the sale contract and keep wire records so the funds are traceable.
- Pre-aliyah buyers (not yet Israeli residents) face stricter terms as foreigners (LTV up to 50 percent, reported). If you plan to make aliyah, discuss the timing with the lawyer and bank.
- Some banks have departments or advisors for English-speaking olim. Ask for an English-speaking advisor.
- Oleh state loan (the Zakaut mortgage, from the Ministry of Construction and Housing): available within 15 years of becoming an oleh; seven banks reportedly issue the eligibility certificate (Teudat Zakaut) and handle it; the rate was described as the Bank of Israel average rate for the term minus 0.5 percent, capped at 3 percent, with no prepayment penalty; the loan size is by a points system. Amounts reported conflict (one source says up to about 300,000 NIS; a bank page says up to 200,000 NIS), so confirm. Eligibility requires not having owned a home in Israel in the past 10 years, per the source.

### Government housing programs

- Mechir LeMishtaken ("Price for the Buyer") and Dira BeHanacha ("Apartment at a Discount") are government lotteries for discounted apartments built on state land. Participation requires an eligibility certificate (Ishur Zakaut) from gov.il, valid for 12 months, and entry into a lottery for specific projects during a registration window. The lottery sets your number; winners choose in order.
- Eligibility generally requires Israeli citizenship or residency and not owning a home in recent years (about six, depending on the wave), with income and household criteria. A February 2026 source says US citizens who are not Israeli residents are not eligible; dual US-Israeli citizens may qualify if they meet residency requirements. An October 2026 guide says olim can buy discounted housing, but it does not confirm priority for olim. Eligibility rules vary by wave: check the official portal (dira.moch.gov.il and gov.il).
- Resale restrictions: usually you cannot freely resell for several years (a guide gives about five years after occupancy permit or seven years from the win date, whichever is earlier). The contract controls.
- Equity needs are lower, but a mortgage is still needed. Purchase tax still applies.
- Another route: Israel Land Authority tenders and "Dira LeHaskir" (rental for 'Dira Lehashkir', a state rental company). The latter is for rental housing.
- Winning a lottery does not guarantee a mortgage. Check your mortgage eligibility early.

## US citizen implications (general; consult a cross-border CPA)

This is awareness only, not advice.
- US citizens and green card holders are taxed on worldwide income. Owning a home in Israel does not by itself create a US reporting form, but related items do:
  - FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) if aggregate foreign financial accounts exceed 10,000 USD at any time in the year. Israeli bank and brokerage accounts count. Mortgage accounts are liabilities, not accounts, but accounts used for payments count.
  - FATCA Form 8938 with your US tax return if foreign financial assets exceed higher thresholds (these vary by filing status and residence). Real property itself is not a specified foreign financial asset, but accounts and certain interests are.
  - Rental income from the property is reported on the US return with foreign tax credits for Israeli tax; the US-Israel tax treaty and the foreign tax credit apply. Depreciation rules differ for foreign property.
  - Selling the property: US capital gain is computed in USD using exchange rates at purchase and sale, which can create taxable currency gains even when shekel gain is small. The home sale exclusion for a principal residence (Section 121) may apply if requirements are met. Israeli mas shevach interacts with the US foreign tax credit.
  - Mortgage in shekels: currency movements can affect the dollar value of the debt.
  - Gifts from parents or family abroad: gifts from foreign persons above a threshold require reporting (Form 3520), and large gifts from US parents may involve US gift tax filings by the donor. Plan the down payment with a tax professional.
  - Israeli investment funds can raise PFIC issues; do not buy Israeli mutual funds without advice.
- Israeli tax residency for olim: new olim have tax benefits on foreign income in the first years after aliyah (a ten-year exemption on some foreign income and a reporting regime has been reported). The specifics are outside this skill. Use a tax advisor familiar with both systems before aliyah.
- Moving money: sending USD to Israel, converting to shekels, and documenting source of funds matter for banks and for anti-money-laundering rules. Cash above reporting thresholds must be declared on entry.
- Banks may require US-person forms (W-9) and may close accounts of US persons in rare cases; ask when opening.

## Risks to name for the user

- Interest-rate and CPI risk on variable tracks. Index risk on new builds.
- Overpaying in an unfamiliar neighborhood; buying under pressure.
- Unregistered or leasehold rights, unauthorized building additions, lien or seller debts.
- New-build contractor delay or failure.
- Currency risk for USD savings and USD income vs shekel payments.
- Tax exposure from either country, especially for US persons.
- Concentration: a home is a large asset in one country and one market.
- Relying on olim benefits that expire.
