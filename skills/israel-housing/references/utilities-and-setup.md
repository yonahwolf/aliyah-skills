# Utilities and Home Setup

Last verified: November 2026. Provider names, phone numbers, and prices change; confirm on the provider's site. Some figures below come from Nefesh B'Nefesh (NBN) guides, whose dates vary.

## Order of operations

1. Before keys: confirm in the lease who pays what and who holds the account.
2. On key day: photograph and read every meter, then transfer accounts.
3. First week: internet, insurance, standing orders, address updates.
4. First month: remaining benefits and registrations (see first-30-days).

## Electricity (Hevrat HaChashmal / IEC)

- The Israel Electric Corporation (IEC) is the main grid operator and the default supplier. Since the electricity market opened, consumers may also choose a private supplier; a Globes report said hundreds of thousands of households have switched. Savings vary; compare carefully before switching and read terms.
- To transfer the account: read the electric meter, then call 103 (IEC's service line) with the contract number (top left of the bill), your name, ID number, and phone, and request a final reading. Bills are issued about every two months, and a one-time transfer fee may appear on the first bill (NBN guide; confirm).
- Even as a tenant, put the account in your name when the lease allows. If left in the landlord's name, you can be left owing for usage without a legal record.
- Electricity capacity: ask about the electrical panel (luach hashmal) and phase. Older apartments may not support several air conditioners and an oven together.
- Billing tips: summer air conditioning can spike bills. Time-of-use plans and private suppliers may reduce costs if you use power at off-peak times.

## Water (municipal water corporation, "taagid mayim")

- Each municipality or regional water corporation bills for water and sewage. Some bills are bundled with arnona; others are separate. Examples named in NBN: Hagihon (Jerusalem), Mei Avivim (Tel Aviv area), Mei Sheva (Beersheba). Look up the corporation's name for your city.
- The bill has personal consumption, a share of common building consumption, and sewage. Bills arrive about every two to three months.
- The olim arnona discount does not apply to water charges (NBN).
- To transfer: read the meter and give the corporation your lease, ID, and meter reading. Procedures vary by city; many now have online forms, others still use fax or in-person service. Check the corporation's website.
- Solar water heater (dud shemesh): hot water in winter often needs the electric boiler turned on 30 to 60 minutes before showers (NBN).

## Gas

- Buildings use a central gas tank or private cylinders. For central gas, an account with the gas company covers usage; with cylinders, track refill dates and order refills.
- Gas providers include Amisragas, Pazgas, Supergas. Contact the one named in your building.
- If you smell gas, leave, open windows, and call the gas company.

## Internet, TV, and mobile

- Internet: two layers. Infrastructure (Bezeq, HOT, or fiber networks) brings the line; an internet service provider (ISP) sells the plan. Fiber is expanding. Check which infrastructure and ISPs reach your address.
- Major providers include Bezeq, HOT, Partner, Cellcom, Golan, and others. Compare current bundles on a comparison site or by calling. Ask about contract length, installation fee, and router rental.
- TV: streaming dominates; Israeli services (HOT, YES, STING TV, Cellcom TV, Partner TV) and global services. Many olim use US streaming plus VPN; check your streaming service terms.
- Mobile: major carriers include Partner, Cellcom, Pelephone, HOT Mobile, Golan Telecom, and 019. Plans are often unlimited data at modest prices. Compare plans, and check eSIM options for arrival. A local number is useful for banking SMS codes, Wolt, and delivery services.
- Keep your US number for US banks if needed (consider a low-cost plan or an eSIM), since two-factor codes may require it.

## Arnona (municipal tax)

- Register the property in your name at the municipality (many cities have online forms): bring the lease or proof of ownership, ID, the last bill, and your olim certificate if claiming the discount. Choose payment: credit card, bank standing order (hora'at keva), or counter.
- Olim discount: customarily 50 to 90 percent depending on the municipality, up to 100 sqm, for one 12-month period used within two years of aliyah, requiring a lease of at least 12 months or proof of ownership (NBN). Rules vary by city, and some cancel the discount if a bill is unpaid. Check with your municipality.
- Other discounts: students, low-income households, new parents, disabled persons, seniors, and others may qualify. Municipal websites list them; some require annual re-application.
- Keep proof of payment and ask the previous tenant for the final bill and a clearance when switching accounts.

## Vaad bayit (building committee)

- A monthly or periodic fee for building upkeep (cleaning, elevator, lighting, gardening, insurance). Ask the landlord or neighbors who collects and how much.
- Large expenses (roof, elevator replacement, painting) may be billed separately. Clarify in the lease who pays them.
- Take part in meetings if you can, to know the building.

## Moving-in checklist

- Day of key handover: video-walk the apartment with the landlord; photograph defects, walls, floors, appliances, and meters with a timestamp; read meters (electric, water, gas if separate).
- Test: all taps (hot and cold), toilets, drains, water pressure, air conditioners, boiler, oven, stove, fridge, outlets, lights, door and window locks, shutters (tris), intercom, mailbox, parking and storage.
- Written list: send defects to the landlord by email or WhatsApp within a day or two, with photos.
- Get: keys, remote controls, mailbox key, parking remote, appliance manuals, vaad bayit contact, landlord's and a repairman's contact, and the building's emergency contacts.
- Locate: main electric panel, water main shutoff, gas shutoff, and the closest shelter or mamad.
- Transfer: electricity, water, gas, arnona, vaad bayit.
- Safety: smoke detector, carbon monoxide detector if gas heating.
- Insurance: home contents, and structure if you own.
- Cleaning and pest check: ask the landlord about prior treatment.

## Standing orders (hora'at keva)

- Set up bank standing orders or credit card auto-pay for arnona, electricity, water, gas, internet, and vaad bayit. Many providers apply discounts for auto-pay.
- The date of withdrawal matters: avoid overdrafts (they carry high interest in Israel). Keep a buffer.
- Cancel old accounts' standing orders when you move.

## Banks, credit cards, and money for olim

- Opening an account: bring your Teudat Zehut, passport, Teudat Oleh, and proof of address; some banks require a visit in person. NBN and the banks offer onboarding for new olim, including some pre-aliyah accounts. Ask about English-speaking branches and a bank's olim package, and compare fees.
- Credit cards: Israeli cards are issued by Isracard, Cal, and Max, tied to banks. Without Israeli credit history, you may start with a debit or a limited credit card. Credit history grows with on-time payments.
- Checkbooks (pinkas shekim) and post-dated checks: often needed for rent. Ask about getting a checkbook early.
- Transfers from abroad: use the bank's incoming wire process, keep documents that show the source, and compare exchange rates and fees with currency transfer services.
- Cash and valuables on arrival: amounts above 50,000 NIS carried into Israel must be declared (NBN customs page).
- US banking: keep a US account and be aware of FBAR and FATCA reporting if you are a US person (see buying reference).

## Renter's and home insurance

- Contents insurance (bituach tochen): covers your belongings, theft, fire, water damage, and sometimes third-party liability. Landlords may require it; ask. Prices vary; compare online.
- Structure insurance (bituach mivne): owners pay it, and mortgage lenders require it, along with life insurance.
- Tenant liability: some policies cover damage you cause to the apartment or neighbors.
- Read exclusions, such as limits on valuables and electronics. Damage from war or terror is generally handled by a state compensation system (the Property Tax and Compensation Fund, Mas Rechush) rather than private policies. Ask your insurer what is and is not covered, and verify the current compensation rules on gov.il.

## Furnishing

- IKEA Israel (delivery and assembly available), local furniture stores, and big-box stores (ACE, Home Center) for hardware and appliances.
- Used: Yad2, Facebook Marketplace, community "Free" and "Buy and Sell" groups (including Anglo groups for each city), and Janglo classifieds. Olim often buy used.
- Appliances: many unfurnished apartments come without a fridge, stove, or washer; check what is included, since air conditioners are often provided and the rest are negotiable.
- Electrical items: Israel uses 230V, 50Hz with a Type H plug (and Type C is also used). Many US appliances are 110V and will not work without a transformer; transformers work for small items but not for heavy appliances. Consider buying locally.
- Shipping furniture from the US is rarely cost-effective for apartments; compare with buying locally.

## Olim customs benefits (verify with Israel Customs / Tax Authority)

Source: NBN page on customs benefits; verify on gov.il.
- Window: three years from aliyah for tax-free household shipments and reduced tax on a vehicle, with extensions for army service, studies, or long time abroad.
- Household shipments: the Customs Authority opens a file after aliyah. Up to three shipments (a container larger than full counts as two unless both arrive together); each shipment can come from any country; postal parcels count too. Clothing and footwear sent by mail within a defined window around arrival are exempt and do not count.
- Tax-free items reported: personal effects, furniture, one of each household appliance, a stated number of computers, TVs, and phones per family, carpets up to a share of the floor area, and air conditioners and heaters relative to the number of rooms. New and used goods rules differ; check before shipping.
- Items needing approvals: large phone counts, e-scooters, and some other goods need ministry approval.
- Medications: need prescriptions and have quantity limits.
- Practical: use an experienced olim-focused shipper, get a written quote, keep an inventory with values, and keep Teudat Oleh and ID ready for release. Courier-paid customs fees may not be refundable.
- The details and thresholds change; call the Customs authority or NBN before shipping.

## Moving companies and shipping

- Use a shipper experienced with aliyah shipments, who knows the Customs release paperwork and clearing agents. Ask for references, insurance coverage, door-to-door vs port-to-port, and delivery timeframe (sea freight takes weeks).
- Local movers: ask in community groups for recommendations; get a written quote with the floor, elevator, and crane-lift needs; confirm insurance.

## Post, address, Bituach Leumi

- Address registration: update your address with the Ministry of Interior (Misrad HaPnim) through its process, because many services link to your registered address in the ID.
- Israel Post (Doar Yisrael): mailing and parcel pickup. Many parcels go to post office lockers or pickup points; set up the Israel Post app and notifications. Provide your mobile number for parcel notices.
- Bituach Leumi (National Insurance): update your address and any account details; check rights to benefits (e.g., child allowance, maternity) via its website. Olim benefits apply.
- Health fund (kupat cholim): choose one and register; notify of address change.
- Other updates: bank, credit cards, employer, school, tax authority, and the municipality.

## First 30 days checklist

Week 1
- Meters read and photographed; defects list sent to landlord.
- Utilities, arnona, and vaad bayit transferred.
- Internet and mobile active.
- Home insurance bound.
- Teudat Oleh handy; Teudat Zehut obtained if not yet received.

Weeks 2 to 3
- Bank account active, standing orders set, checkbook ordered if needed.
- Health fund registered.
- Municipality arnona discount application submitted (within the eligibility window).
- Customs file confirmed and shipping arranged.
- Address updated with Ministry of Interior and Bituach Leumi.
- Rental assistance filed if eligible (confirm timing with the ministry).

Weeks 3 to 4
- Furnish essentials and shop for appliances.
- Meet the neighbors and vaad bayit; learn the building's rules.
- Learn the nearest shelter, clinic, pharmacy, and supermarket.
- Set a recurring calendar reminder for lease renewal, check expirations, and benefit deadlines.
- Ulpan or other integration steps if applicable (outside this skill).
