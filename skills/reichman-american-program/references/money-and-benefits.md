# Money, benefits, visa status, and army

Last verified: November 2026. Figures are 2026-27 unless noted. All amounts "subject to change" per Reichman. Currency: Reichman quotes tuition in USD; Israeli government benefits are in NIS. Do not convert at a guessed rate; use a current rate.

## Tuition and fees (RRIS undergraduate)

- 2026-27 first-year tuition: two tiers, about $18,200 and $18,500 per year depending on program, plus an optional $75 student union fee (opt-out by signing a form by about November 25). Which program sits in which tier was not clear in the document; ask.
- For comparison, 2025-26 tuition was $16,900 and $17,200 (plus $60 union fee), showing tuition rises year to year. Plan for further increases in 2027-28 and later years (unverified).
- Payment (2026-27): $1,050 down payment (counts toward tuition) then installments on the 10th of November through April. Pay by credit card (USD in one payment) or wire; bank fees are yours.
- Tuition excludes: flights, textbooks, dorm rent, living expenses, and (if you buy it) insurance.
- Health insurance requirement: all students need coverage. The university plan (Harel Yedidim) cost 4,970 NIS for 2026-27 (Oct 1 to Sep 30); it does not cover pre-existing conditions. Israeli residents covered by Bituach Leumi (National Insurance) do not need extra insurance.
- Refund schedule penalizes late withdrawal: by mid-July no charge, then graduated charges on the down payment through October, then 50 percent of tuition after late October, 66 percent from late January, 100 percent from mid-March (2025-26 schedule). Freezing studies is treated as withdrawal and cancels scholarships retroactively. Ask for the current schedule.
- Living costs: Reichman's 2025-26 estimate was about $600 to $880 per month for food, transport, and basics (excluding housing). Tel Aviv-area rents are high; see `life-logistics.md`.
- Total first-year budget formula: tuition + insurance + dorm or rent (12 months, contract to Sept 30) + living + flights + books. Do the arithmetic for the user with current figures and a stated exchange rate.

## Reichman scholarships and aid

- **Merit scholarship** (RRIS): awarded automatically to new applicants on prior academic achievement; you cannot apply directly. The 2025-26 rules say 50 percent of first-year tuition, renewable for one more year with a GPA of at least 88 and 40 volunteer hours. Combined merit plus need-based aid cannot exceed 75 percent of tuition. Full-time students only. Repayment is required if studies end (including leave). Recipients write a donor thank-you letter yearly. Check the current-year rules; how many students receive it and the threshold for it were not stated.
- **Needs-based scholarship** (RRIS): a supplemental scholarship, not full tuition, with limited funds. For 2026-27 first-years the booklet gave an application deadline of October 18, 2026; the document itself said to apply as early as possible and that incomplete applications are not reviewed. Eligibility: RRIS students who lived abroad until the start of the year or have lived in Israel under five years (army does not count), or oleh students under five years in Israel, or continuing scholarship recipients. Not eligible: part-time or exchange students, students whose rent exceeds dorm cost, students who exceed the 3-year program, students who own real estate (owning a car requires a check), students with unfulfilled community service, those who failed more than three courses or have an average below 60, and students with a disciplinary finding. Documents: personal letter, FAFSA Student Aid Index for US students, lease, three months of bank statements and salary slips for student and parents, parents' tax return, spouse documents if married. Students must first pursue other aid (Student Authority grant, Masa, other grants and loans). Contact: rris.financialaid@runi.ac.il.
- Timing note: the needs-based deadline in the booklet falls after the semester starts, so you may be admitted without knowing your aid. Ask admissions when aid decisions come relative to the deposit.
- **Honors programs** (Argov, Zell, Adelson honors): may carry additional scholarships; not verified.
- Graduate students: Reichman FAQ says scholarships are for undergraduates, not graduate students.

## US federal aid

- Reichman's consumer information policy (2025-26) lists the Raphael Recanati International School under FAFSA school code G40703 and describes Direct Subsidized, Direct Unsubsidized, and Direct PLUS loans for eligible US citizens/eligible non-citizens, plus a Title IV refund policy. It also mentions private loans (Sallie Mae). The 2024 one-pager says FAFSA can be used only for loans (no Pell or federal grants in practice), and that 529 plan funds and the Masa grant can be used. Verify the current FAFSA code, loan limits, interest rates for the new award year, and eligibility (SSN, Selective Service registration for males) on studentaid.gov and with Reichman's financial aid office.
- A FAFSA is also required for the needs-based scholarship (Student Aid Index).
- Practical note: federal loans are credit-based for PLUS (parents) and capped for undergraduates; they do not cover full tuition plus living. Plan a funding stack.

## Student Authority (Minhal HaStudentim) benefit for olim

For new immigrants (Ministry of Aliyah and Integration). Summary of Nefesh B'Nefesh's description; confirm on the Student Authority site.
- Eligible: start studies within 36 months of aliyah (army, national service, and Masa time do not count against the window). Age at start up to 27 for undergraduate and mechina, up to 30 for graduate programs.
- Covers studies that start after the aliyah date. Covers the standard length of the degree (about 3 to 4 years for a BA). Pays up to the standard state tuition rate: about 11,296 NIS per year undergraduate, 15,265 NIS graduate, about 17,800 NIS for mechina, up to 4,500 NIS for required language studies.
- Key point: the amount is a fixed cap tied to Israeli public tuition. RRIS tuition is several times higher, so the benefit offsets only part of RRIS tuition. English-language and international programs often cost more than the standard rate and may not be fully covered, and you pay the difference.
- Does not cover dorms, living expenses, registration fees, insurance. Universities reimburse students after proof of payment.
- Application: open an account before aliyah if possible; after aliyah apply with your teudat oleh and teudat zehut, transcripts, acceptance letter, tuition receipt. Deadlines for new students: November 10 (first semester), April 1 (second), August 15 (summer). Reichman tells new immigrants to update the Student Authority with their academic status.
- Usually cannot be combined with another government grant for the same year, and long-term recipients complete a volunteer program (Shahak).
- Implication for planning: an American who makes aliyah and then starts RRIS can get this benefit; an American on an A/2 student visa cannot (not an oleh). The timing of aliyah relative to start date matters; see below.

## Student visa (A/2) vs aliyah (oleh) status

| Item | A/2 student visa | Oleh (Law of Return) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Foreign national enrolled at an Israeli institution | Jewish (or eligible relatives under the Law of Return), approved by the Ministry of Interior/Jewish Agency or consulate |
| Duration | Reichman's booklet says a one-year multiple-entry visa, renewable. Nefesh B'Nefesh says valid up to three years then renewable; the sources differ, so confirm with the consulate | Permanent resident and citizen rights on arrival |
| Tuition | Standard RRIS tuition | No separate olim tuition rate was found in the 2026-27 documents; plus Student Authority benefit and olim-only scholarships (needs-based criteria include new-immigrant status) |
| Work | May work part-time if eligible under Law of Return and it does not interfere with studies (per Nefesh B'Nefesh); otherwise restricted | Full work rights; tax credit points for new immigrants |
| Health care | Must buy insurance (university plan or other) | Must join a Kupat Cholim (health fund) and pay health tax; the insurance requirement for RRIS is met by Bituach Leumi coverage (per booklet) |
| Army | Not conscripted as a foreign citizen on a student visa (general rule; verify) | Age-of-arrival rules apply (below) |

- Reichman says students born in Israel, new immigrants, and students with an Israeli parent should contact the consulate and RRIS about their specific status. Anyone born abroad to an Israeli parent is generally considered an Israeli citizen already; they cannot get a student visa, and issuing a teudat zehut counts as aliyah.
- Converting from student visa to oleh status after arrival is possible for eligible people; the exact process was not verified from an official page. Contact the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration, Nefesh B'Nefesh, or the Jewish Agency. Ask specifically how the date of aliyah affects Student Authority eligibility (studies must start after aliyah), the 36-month window, and army age.
- Proof of Jewish status is the practical gate: Nefesh B'Nefesh notes rabbi's letters or conversion certificates are used for the Interior Ministry. Non-Jewish students and those without Law of Return eligibility remain on A/2 status.

## Masa and other grants

- Reichman's financial pages mention the Masa Israel grant for eligible students; Masa funds long-term Israel programs for young Jewish adults abroad. Whether and how much Masa gives to a full degree student at RRIS (as opposed to a study-abroad semester or year) could not be verified. Masa's own page only confirmed a subsidy for accepted students in Masa-affiliated study programs. Check eligibility with Masa and RRIS financial aid, including age limit and whether olim are eligible (not verified; Masa grants are designed for programs by people who have not made aliyah, so confirm).
- Other: foundation and municipal scholarships, low-interest student loans for olim, and voucher programs (Nefesh B'Nefesh notes government help can fund either vocational training or higher education at one time). Ask about donor-specific scholarships (Zell, Argov, Adelson) and US community scholarships for studying in Israel.
- 529 plans: Reichman states 529 funds may be used (2024 one-pager); verify that Reichman is eligible by its Title IV status and check IRS rules for your plan.

## Army and IDF service

- Rules depend on age on arrival and status. For olim who arrive at age 18-19: single men serve about 32 months and single women 24 months; at 20-21: men 24 months and women 24 months; at 22-27: no mandatory service (volunteering is possible; women may serve 18 or 32 months depending on role; men 18 or 24 months); at 28 and older: exempt and not eligible. Married people, medical professions, and those who arrived before June 2015 follow different tables. Nefesh B'Nefesh notes these rules are being updated. Confirm with the lone-soldier team (lspen@nbn.org.il) and the IDF.
- "Age of arrival" can differ from age at aliyah, because it reflects the start of a significant stay in Israel. Anyone who plans aliyah near age 18 to 21, or lives in Israel for a long period, should ask before arrival how long stays count.
- A foreign citizen on an A/2 student visa generally is not drafted. If the same person becomes an oleh and a resident, the draft rules attach. The relationship to academic deferral (atuda) or delaying until after the degree was not verified; ask NBN and the IDF before choosing a status for a student aged 18 to 21.
- Mechina has two meanings: (a) university preparatory program (academic, English/Hebrew/math bridging), and (b) pre-military mechina (a gap-year program before service). This skill concerns mainly (a). Do not mix them up.
- The need-based scholarship excludes army years from the five-year count.
- Alternatives: Garin Tzabar, Mahal (volunteers from abroad), and Sherut Leumi are separate; they are not Reichman programs. Mention only if asked and point to NBN and the IDF.

## Taxes and olim financial benefits

- New immigrants get extra income-tax credit points on Israeli employment income for up to 4.5 years after arrival, and a long exemption on foreign income for 10 years (arrivals from January 1, 2026 must still report foreign income), per the Israel Tax Authority guide valid for 2026. Credit points are worth 2,904 NIS per year each in that guide.
- The guide did not mention a student-specific tax credit. Whether a student has Israeli income worth claiming credits depends on work; ask an accountant or the Tax Authority.
- Aliyah brings a "sal klita" (absorption basket), ulpan eligibility, municipal tax discounts, and other benefits; amounts change. See Nefesh B'Nefesh and the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration (links in `sources.md`).
- US citizens living abroad still file US tax returns; the foreign earned income exclusion and tax treaty questions are for a tax professional.
