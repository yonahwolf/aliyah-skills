# Programs for English-speaking and American students

Last verified: November 2026. Sources: BA admissions regulations 2026-27, RRIS acceptance booklet, North America one-pager (2024), BA brochure 2027-28. See `sources.md`.

## The key fact: RRIS is the English-taught track

The Raphael Recanati International School (RRIS) is the track for international and English-speaking students. Per Reichman, its bachelor's programs are taught entirely in English and take three years (BA or BSc). Reichman's other schools also teach in Hebrew; for most students from the US the RRIS route is the realistic one.

You can apply to only one program at a time, and admission criteria differ by program.

## Hebrew-taught programs (not RRIS)

Reichman's schools (Arison, Lauder, Efi Arazi, Sammy Ofer, Adelson, Ivcher, Tiomkin) also run Hebrew-language degrees for Israeli students. Admission there generally means Israeli-style criteria (Bagrut and psychometric exam, or equivalents) and fluent academic Hebrew. Their exact requirements for overseas applicants were not verified. If a student has strong Hebrew (for example, a gap year plus ulpan, or a Hebrew-speaking home), they can ask admissions whether a Hebrew-track program is open to them and what Hebrew exam or placement is used. Most American students do not choose this route for the first degree.

## Special and honors tracks

Reichman lists special programs including the Argov Fellows Program in Leadership, the Zell Entrepreneurship Program, the Adelson School of Entrepreneurship honors program, and an English-taught Public Diplomacy honors program for third-year government students (per the 2027-28 brochure). These are selective add-ons with their own applications. Their benefits, eligibility, and deadlines were not retrievable, so check https://www.runi.ac.il/en/special-programs and ask admissions whether you apply with the RRIS application or separately.

## Law, medicine, and other professional paths

- Harry Radzyner Law School is taught in Hebrew (the brochure says many courses are available in English). Not an RRIS option.
- Dina Recanati School of Medicine is Hebrew-taught (four-year MD for bachelor's graduates). A Life Sciences preparatory program for medical studies exists with Hebrew and English tracks, with its own admissions regulations and tuition.
- Treat any plan involving US licensing (medicine, law) as a separate research project; Israeli professional degrees do not automatically qualify for US licensure.

## Preparatory and bridging options

- **English preparation**: students whose English test results place them low take a summer English preparatory course (Aug 10 to 31, 2026 in the last cycle) and extra English courses in year one.
- **Math preparation**: online math refresher and Math for Computer Science / Math for Business courses before the semester, if required in the acceptance letter (summer 2026 dates were late June to early September). Attendance is mandatory when required.
- **Hebrew**: optional summer intensive ulpan (about five weeks, extra fee, no Reichman credit) and a yearlong elective ulpan for two elective credits. Not required for RRIS admission.
- **Academic mechina (university preparatory year)**: relevant mainly for olim who lack a qualifying high-school credential for Hebrew-track study; the Student Authority can fund mechina when a university requires it. Not described in RRIS materials; ask.

## Graduate options (for a graduating college senior)

Reichman's English-taught graduate offerings found during research include a One-Year MBA, an MSc in Machine Learning and Data Science, and an MA in Government (RRIS graduate admissions). Others exist. Graduate admission, tuition, and scholarships differ from undergraduate (the FAQ says scholarships are for undergraduates, not graduate students). Start at https://www.runi.ac.il/en/schools/rris/admissions/graduate and verify each program's language, length, and cost. For an aspiring oleh, one-year programs matter for the Student Authority, which funds up to twice the graduate rate over the program, with half paid after graduation (see `money-and-benefits.md`).

## When Reichman may not be the best fit

- The student wants a broad liberal-arts education, undecided major, or the ability to switch majors freely.
- The student wants a large residential campus with traditional US college life, big-time sports, and Greek life.
- The family needs full-need-based aid at US levels; Reichman's aid is supplemental.
- The student needs a Jerusalem-style or Torah-integrated setting; consider Hebrew University, Bar-Ilan, Jerusalem College of Technology, and others.
- The student wants an engineering or science degree with labs and research depth; Technion and the larger research universities are strong alternatives.
