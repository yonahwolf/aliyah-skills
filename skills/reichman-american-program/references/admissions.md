# Admissions: RRIS undergraduate

Last verified: November 2026. Primary source: BA application and admissions regulations 2026-27 and RRIS acceptance booklet 2026-27. Dates below are for the 2026-27 cycle. The 2027-28 cycle should follow a similar pattern but its dates were not confirmed; check the live page.

## Cycle and deadlines

- Application window for 2026-27 ran from November 1, 2025 to July 15, 2026, with all documents due by July 15. By pattern, the 2027-28 window likely opens around November 2026 and closes mid-July 2027 (unverified).
- Admissions are rolling. Reichman says applications are not reviewed until complete, and decisions typically come within about two weeks of a complete file.
- Some programs can fill before the deadline, and applicants may be waitlisted. Late applications are reviewed only if spots remain and may face stricter criteria.
- Start of the academic year: fall semester began October 18, 2026 (orientation October 11 to 16). Spring semester starts March 7, 2027. Expect a similar mid-October start for 2027-28.
- Applying early also helps with scholarships, visa timing, and dorm space.

## Application steps

1. Choose one program (you can apply to only one at a time).
2. Complete the online application form and pay the non-refundable $100 USD application fee.
3. Upload documents (list below). Recommenders send letters directly.
4. If required for your program, take or submit SAT/ACT, the English proficiency exam, math evaluation, or other forms.
5. The admissions committee may invite you to an interview and may give some preference to those interviewed. Format was not described in the regulations.
6. Receive a decision. If accepted, confirm and pay the enrollment deposit through the Candidate Information Station (the 2026 booklet gave $1,050 and asked for it within 7 days; it counts toward annual tuition).
7. Follow the acceptance booklet: update personal details, insurance, preparatory courses, dorm registration, visa, scholarships, orientation.

Track status in the Candidate Information Station (the applicant portal). Admissions staff are reachable at rris.registrar@runi.ac.il, +972-9-9602700, or for North America rris.us@runi.ac.il and +1-866-999-RRIS.

## Required documents

- Scanned passport or Israeli ID.
- Official high school transcripts and diploma. Applicants with 30 or more college credits may submit those instead of high school transcripts, but quantitative programs still require high school transcripts.
- Two letters of recommendation (English or Hebrew), sent by the recommender directly. Typical choices: a teacher, counselor, or employer. Pick people who can speak to ability and character.
- A 250 to 500 word English essay on why you want to study at Reichman and in your chosen program.
- English CV or resume with full name at the top; list any military service.
- Proof of English proficiency if your high school was not English-medium.
- Program-specific items: math evaluation form from a high school math teacher for Computer Science and Entrepreneurship and Computer Science; a supplementary questionnaire for Entrepreneurship.
- Israelis: eligibility for the Bagrut certificate (unless already holding a degree or over 30).

## Tests

- **SAT/ACT**: optional for 2026-27 for applicants from US or Israeli high schools, and applicants are not penalized for not testing. Required for Computer Science, and required for US applicants whose high school math grades fall below the university's minimum (the minimum was not published on the page). Strongly recommended for non-US applicants who fall short on math grades. Waived for transfer students with at least one year of university study with honors or distinction. If you test, the codes in the regulations are SAT 4267 and ACT 5385. Reichman publishes a table converting SAT/ACT to the Israeli psychometric scale (for example, SAT 1400 corresponds to psychometric 700 and ACT 31) for reference.
- **Psychometric exam**: optional for Israeli high school graduates; results valid ten years and sent directly from NITE. Not needed by most American applicants to RRIS, but relevant if applying to Hebrew-track programs or other Israeli universities (the psychometric is offered in English a few times a year per Nefesh B'Nefesh).
- Check each year whether "test-optional" still applies. This policy is explicitly year-specific.

## English proficiency

- Required if your high school was not primarily English-medium. Accepted exams listed: TOEFL, IELTS, Pearson PTE, Cambridge CAE/CPE, and the Israeli Amirnet. If retaking, wait at least 35 days.
- Results place you in one of four levels: exempt; Advanced II (one English course in year one); Advanced I and II (two courses); English preparatory course (summer course before the year plus two English courses). TOEFL code in the regulations: 0927.
- Native English speakers from English-medium schools are in practice exempt, but confirm.

## Hebrew

The RRIS regulations and FAQ do not set a Hebrew requirement for admission. Hebrew is optional (see ulpan options in `programs.md`). Hebrew-track programs would require Hebrew; requirements for those were not verified.

## Transfer students and credit

- Credit is evaluated only after you confirm enrollment and pay the deposit; transcripts and English syllabi go through the Candidate Information Station.
- Only courses completed in the last five years are considered. Up to 40 percent of prior credits may transfer per the FAQ, subject to fit with course requirements. At least 60 percent of credits and at least two academic years must be earned at Reichman.
- Transfer is not guaranteed, and credits do not necessarily shorten the three-year degree.
- Implication: a student with some college credits should ask for a preliminary view before committing, but should not count on shortening the degree.

## Other pathways

- Exchange and study-abroad: semester and year programs exist for students from partner or other universities (separate tuition; semester about $12,900 and year about $25,800 for 2026-27 per the study-abroad fee sheet). Not a degree program. A semester abroad is a low-risk way to try Israel.
- Students who have already served in the IDF, are Israeli, or hold Israeli credentials may have other options; ask admissions.

## Questions to email admissions (copy and adapt)

1. For the [year] entry, what are the application opening date, final deadline, and last date for scholarship consideration?
2. For [program], is SAT/ACT required or recommended for me, given my math grades? What is the minimum math grade or score?
3. Is there an interview, and what is its format?
4. Given my [high school / English medium / IB / AP / dual enrollment], do I need an English proficiency exam?
5. How many credits could transfer from [college], and can I get an informal pre-assessment?
6. What are first-year tuition, fees, and insurance for my status (US citizen on student visa / oleh / dual citizen)?
7. Which scholarships apply automatically at admission, and which need a separate application? Can I stack them with Student Authority or Masa funding?
8. Are Federal Direct Loans available for my program, and what is the process?
9. What do you advise for dorm registration timing and the A/2 visa?
10. If I make aliyah before or during the program, how does that change tuition, scholarship, and visa steps?
