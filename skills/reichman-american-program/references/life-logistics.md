# Life logistics: moving to Herzliya

Last verified: November 2026. Reichman-specific facts (dorms, insurance, visa, orientation) are from the 2026-27 acceptance booklet. General Israel practicalities (banking, phone, transport, kosher, religious life) are common knowledge-level guidance and were not checked against official sources; verify current details with Reichman's student services, Nefesh B'Nefesh, and providers. Other skills in this repo cover housing and Hebrew in more depth.

## Housing

- **Reichman dorms** on campus: 748 units in three towers (per the 2026 booklet). Monthly rents (2026-27): single about $1,060; roommate about $920; couples about $1,670; shared apartments in the Ronson buildings about $910. Rent includes Wi-Fi, cable TV, maintenance, cleaning of common areas, and 24/7 security; electricity and water cost extra. Contract runs from move-in until September 30 of the following year, so you pay for the full academic year plus summer months. Register through the dorm office (dorms@runi.ac.il) only after acceptance and deposit; placement depends on availability. The dorm is a separate entity with its own registration and cancellation terms, so read the cancellation rules before signing.
- **Off campus**: Reichman's alternative-housing page lists LiveHerz (student platform), Student Housing Herzliya, Sabbatical Homes, Tel Aviv student housing providers, and Facebook groups (RRIS classes, Herzliya housing, Janglo). Prices are not published there. Herzliya and Tel Aviv rents are high by US standards; expect to share apartments. Beware of deposits and contracts in Hebrew; have a Hebrew-speaking friend or advisor review. See `israel-housing` skill in this repo for deeper guidance.
- Housing is a major lever for the needs-based scholarship: students whose rent exceeds the dorm cost are not eligible.
- Scholarship paperwork needs a lease; plan housing before the aid application.

## Transportation

- Herzliya has a train station and bus lines to Tel Aviv, and many students commute without a car. Public transportation can run on Shabbat only in limited ways (most intercity service pauses from Friday afternoon to Saturday evening), which affects weekend plans. Use Rav-Kav (the national transit card) and the Moovit or Google Maps apps. Ask at orientation whether student fare discounts apply to you (not verified).
- Owning a car in Israel is expensive and requires a license conversion; the needs-based scholarship asks students who own a car to contact the aid office.

## Money and banking

- Many students open an Israeli bank account after arrival; olim have a smoother path with teudat zehut and teudat oleh. Students on A/2 visas can usually open accounts but processes vary by bank. Ask the bank about foreign-student requirements before relying on it.
- US cards work; consider a card with no foreign-transaction fee. Plan for how tuition, rent deposits, and scholarship refunds move in USD and NIS. International wires take about a week to confirm at Reichman.
- Keep US banking, credit, and tax filing in good order while abroad.

## Phone and internet

- Prepaid and low-cost plans are available from Israeli carriers; an unlocked US phone with an Israeli SIM or eSIM is the typical choice. Dorm Wi-Fi is included. WhatsApp is the default communication tool for classes, clubs, and landlords.

## Health insurance and care

- All students must have coverage. Options: Reichman's plan (Harel Yedidim, 4,970 NIS per year in 2026-27, Oct 1 to Sep 30, excludes pre-existing conditions, health declaration required starting about six weeks before the policy begins) or your own comprehensive plan. Residents covered by Bituach Leumi do not need extra insurance.
- Olim must join a Kupat Cholim (HMO: Clalit, Maccabi, Meuhedet, or Leumit) and pay health tax through Bituach Leumi; details and any age-based rules for students were not verified. Ask NBN or the Ministry.
- Bring a supply of prescription medication for the first few months and carry prescriptions typed in English, as the booklet advises. Plan mental-health care too; ask about campus counseling services.

## Language

- RRIS classes are in English. Daily life (bureaucracy, landlords, shops) uses Hebrew, though English is widely spoken in Herzliya and Tel Aviv. Options: Reichman summer intensive ulpan (about five weeks, extra fee, no credit), yearlong elective ulpan (two elective credits, placement test in orientation week), government-subsidized ulpan for olim (Nefesh B'Nefesh suggests a subsidized five-month ulpan), apps, and the `hebrew-practice` skill in this repo.

## Food and kosher

- Israel has abundant kosher food. Kosher at Reichman: confirm campus cafeteria and dorm kitchen kashrut arrangements with the university (not verified). Many dorm units have kitchens. Supermarket kosher labeling is standard. Eating out varies; Shabbat closures affect shopping and restaurants (many shops in Tel Aviv-area close or limit hours on Shabbat).

## Religious life

- Herzliya has synagogues across the spectrum (Orthodox, Conservative/Masorti, Reform/Progressive). Reichman is a secular institution: campus is closed on Shabbat and holidays; there are no required religious elements. Options for Jewish learning, Shabbat meals, and community through local programs, Chabad, Hillel-like groups, and Masorti/Reform congregations exist. For non-Jewish students or students of other faiths, the secular campus is workable but ask about options. Do not recommend a religious setting; help the student find what fits.
- The academic calendar does not align with US Jewish and Christian holidays; Christmas, Thanksgiving, and US holidays are not days off.

## Safety and security

- Conditions can change quickly. Ask the user to read the current US State Department travel advisory for Israel, enroll in STEP, review Reichman's emergency procedures (shelter locations and the Home Front Command app are standard practice in Israel), and agree on a family communication plan. Do not offer predictions.

## Arrival timeline (typical; adapt to the entry year)

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| 12 to 9 months before | Research programs; decide test plan (SAT/ACT, English exam); ask aliyah-vs-student-status questions; talk to the army/Student Authority contacts if relevant |
| 9 to 6 months | Draft essay and CV; request recommendation letters; take tests; apply early (rolling admission) |
| By the deadline (mid-July in 2026 cycle) | All documents complete |
| Within days of acceptance | Confirm and pay the deposit; update details in the Candidate Information Station |
| 4 to 3 months before start | Register for dorm; choose insurance; apply for A/2 student visa at an Israeli consulate (or file for aliyah and Student Authority account if applicable); apply for FAFSA loans and needs-based scholarship per deadlines |
| July to September | Preparatory courses if required (English, math, programming); optional summer ulpan; book flights flexibly (exam schedules are individualized and breaks are short) |
| Early October | Move in; Orientation Week (mandatory); set up bank, phone, Rav-Kav, RUNI email |
| Mid-October | Fall semester starts; register for ulpan elective in first two weeks |
| Throughout year | Needs-based and Student Authority deadlines (Nov 10 for new students first semester; April 1 second semester); volunteer hours for scholarship; visa renewals |

## Pre-departure checklist

- Passport valid 6 months beyond stay; visa or oleh documents ready.
- Original or certified birth certificate, acceptance letter, proof of funds, proof of insurance for the visa (per booklet list; consulate may ask more).
- Transcripts and syllabi copies for later credit evaluation back in the US.
- Immunization and medical records; prescriptions in English; adapters and electronics.
- Power of attorney, bank access, and a plan for US mail and taxes.
- Contacts: Reichman admissions, dorm office, financial aid office, consulate.

## Getting credits and records back to the US

- Order official transcripts and a grading-scale explanation each year, not only at graduation.
- Keep all syllabi, reading lists, and course descriptions; US schools may ask.
- For graduate school, ask the target program early about its policy for 3-year degrees; consider a WES or ECE evaluation.
- If the plan is to transfer to a US college, ask the US school beforehand which Reichman courses it will accept; Reichman does not control that decision.
