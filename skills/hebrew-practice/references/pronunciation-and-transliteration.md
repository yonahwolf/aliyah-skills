# Pronunciation and transliteration

**Claude cannot hear audio.** It cannot judge how the learner sounds, hear stress, or catch an accent. What it can do: choose a consistent transliteration, explain sounds and common English-speaker traps, notice spelling and transliteration choices that reveal a sound problem, and point to audio. For real pronunciation feedback, send the learner to native audio (Forvo, Pimsleur, easy-Hebrew radio and news, a tutor or ulpan); see `resources.md`. If the learner shares a dictation transcript, treat it as text: a transcript tells you what the speech tool understood, not whether the pronunciation was good.

## Transliteration scheme used in this skill

Vowels: a (patach, kamatz), e (tzeire, segol; also the quick vowel in ledaber), i, o (cholam), u (shuruk, kubutz). No schwa-reduced sounds: Hebrew vowels stay clear.

Stress marking: in the reference tables, hyphens split syllables and CAPS marks the stressed syllable (ef-SHAR, BO-ker). In running correction lines, mark only words stressed before the last syllable (BO-ker, KE-lev, la-SHE-vet); unmarked words are assumed to be stressed on the last syllable.

Hyphenation of prefixes (be-, le-, ha-, ve-, she-) is only a reading aid. Write it as a hyphen when it clarifies (ve-yesh, be-seder).

## Sounds English speakers get wrong

- **ח (ch) vs ה (h):** chalav (milk, חלב) vs ha-lev (the heart, הלב). Say it from the back of the throat, like clearing the throat softly, not "h".
- **כ (kh) vs כּ/ק (k):** most Israelis say ח and כ-without-dot the same; learn the spelling, say the sound. kh/ch are not "ck" or "sh".
- **ר (r):** mainstream Israeli Hebrew uses a back-of-throat r, similar to French/German, not the English r. A rolled or tapped r is understood and common in some speakers' speech. Do not require one.
- **ע and א:** mainstream speech makes them nearly the same (a small stop or a vowel start). Some speakers pronounce ע as a deep guttural. Both are acceptable.
- **צ (tz):** "ts" even at the start of a word (tzfat, tzvi). English speakers often insert a vowel (tuh-zvi).
- **Pure vowels:** English reduces unstressed vowels to a lazy "uh" (schwa). In Hebrew, "a", "e", "o", "u" stay distinct even when quick. "Efshar" is not "uhf-SHAR", and "toda" is not "tuh-DAH". Short quick vowels (shva na) are the one place a very short "e" appears (le-daber).
- **u:** "u" as in "put" or "too", not "yoo". "Yulia", "kupa".
- **Final stress:** most Hebrew words stress the last syllable (to-DA, ef-SHAR, shalom, kafe). A minority stress the second to last: segolate nouns (BA-yit, KE-lev, SE-fer, DE-let, BO-ker, E-rev), many feminine present forms (me-da-BE-ret, ho-LE-khet), forms with -a'a/-a'at (yo-DE-a, yo-DA-at), and some foreign words (a-ME-ri-ka, KI-lo). When teaching, mark the stressed syllable in new words.
- **Stress can change meaning** in a few forms, for example present and past of "ba'a" (she comes, she came). Tell the learner to ask an Israeli or check an audio source.
- **Diphthongs:** "ai" like "eye" (shalom aleikhem), "ei" like "day". Avoid gliding like English "o" (go). Hebrew "o" is steady.

## Short drills Claude can run (text-based)

- Pairs to read aloud: chalav (milk) / ha-lev (the heart); kelev (dog, KE-lev) / chalav (milk); im (עם, with) and im (אם, if) sound the same.
- Tongue-warmups: "shalom, ma shlomkha?" (sh, l, m), "toda raba" (r), "tzvi halakh la-tza-FON" (tz; ask a native speaker to confirm any new sentence).
- Read-aloud routine: type the sentence in script, then say it three times, slow, normal, fast; record yourself and compare with native audio (Forvo for single words, Pimsleur for phrases).

## Writing without vowels

Everyday Hebrew is written without nikud. Short vowels are often omitted and spelling uses full ("male") and defective ("chaser") forms; the same consonants can mean different words (שמן shemen / shamen). Reading practice: start with vowelized texts (children's books, easy-Hebrew news) and move to unvowelized text. Tell the learner that transliteration can look different from the script spelling; "ani rotza" is spelled with the same letters for both genders.

## How to give pronunciation feedback honestly

- Praise what transliteration shows ("you spelled ch, so you're thinking of the throat sound").
- Flag likely trouble spots from the word shape (a word starting with ח, ר, or צ).
- Never claim to have "heard" them. Say "from what you typed I can't tell how it sounds; record it and compare with a native speaker."
- Suggest one concrete audio action each session.
