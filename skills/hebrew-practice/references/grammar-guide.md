# Grammar guide for correcting English speakers

Use this to explain a fix in one or two sentences, not to lecture. Teach the pattern behind the error only once per session; afterwards just point to it ("gender again - rotza").

Transliteration: ח=ch, כ=kh, צ=tz; stress is on the last syllable unless a syllable is in CAPS. m = masculine, f = feminine, pl = plural.

## 1. Gender (the biggest source of errors)

- Every noun is masculine or feminine; verbs, adjectives, "you", and numbers agree.
- Feminine nouns usually end in -a (ה) or -t (ת): ima (mom), dira (apartment), mita (bed), makhonit (car, מכונית), sha'a (hour/time).
- Common exceptions: **yad** (hand), **regel** (leg/foot), **ayin** (eye), **ir** (city), **eretz** (country), **shemesh** (sun) are feminine without an ending; **laila** (night, לילה) is masculine despite ending in -a.
- The speaker's gender matters for "I": a woman says "ani lomedet", a man "ani lomed". In the **past and future** the verb form with "ani" is the same for men and women (halakhti = I went); in the **present** it differs.
- "You" has four forms: ata (m sg), at (f sg), atem (m/mixed pl), aten (f pl; often replaced by atem in speech).
- Many present-tense forms are spelled the same for m and f (רוצה, שותה, מחכה, רואה, מגיעה); only the vowels differ. Show nikud for the first example: רוֹצֶה rotze (m) / רוֹצָה rotza (f).

## 2. Plurals

- Masculine usually -im, feminine usually -ot: yeled / yeladim (child/children, boy), yalda / yeladot (girl/girls), sefer / sfarim (book/books).
- Mixed or unknown group: masculine plural.
- Irregular but common: ish / anashim (man/people), isha / nashim (woman/women), yom / yamim (day/days), shana / shanim (year, feminine with -im), shulchan / shulchanot (table, masculine with -ot), makom / mekomot (place), ben / banim (son/sons), bat / banot (daughter/daughters).

## 3. Definite article and "et"

- "The" is the prefix **ha-** (ה): bayit / ha-bayit (a house / the house). There is no word for "a".
- Adjectives repeat it: ha-bayit ha-gadol (the big house).
- **et** (את) marks a **definite direct object**: "ani ro'e et ha-yeled" (I see the boy), "ani ro'e et Dan". With indefinite objects, no et: "ani ro'e yeled". Forgetting et is very common and understood; correct it at levels 1-2 gently and consistently.
- Prepositions merge with ha-: be + ha = ba (babayit, in the house), le + ha = la (labayit), me + ha = me-ha (mehabayit).
- With proper names no ha-: "be-Tel Aviv", "le-Yerushalayim".

## 4. Adjectives

- Follow the noun and agree in gender, number, and definiteness: bayit gadol, dira gdola, yeladim ktanim, yeladot ktanot. "The big apartment" = ha-dira ha-gdola.
- With "ze / zot" in short sentences the adjective stays unmarked for the noun: "ze tov" (it's good). Do not say "ani tov" for "I'm fine"; Israelis say "ani be-seder" or "tov li".

## 5. Present tense

- Four forms: m sg, f sg, m pl, f pl. No separate "to be" in the present.
- Pi'el verb ledaber (to speak): medaber / medaberet / medabrim / medabrot.
- Pa'al verb likhtov (to write): kotev / kotevet / kotvim / kotvot.
- Very common: rotze/rotza (want), ohev/ohevet (like, love), yode'a/yoda'at (know), holekh/holekhet (go, walk), ochel/ochelet (eat), shote/shota (drink), oved/ovedet (work), gar/gara (live), lomed/lomedet (study), tzarikh/tzrikha (need).
- Pattern for "ani lo" + present: "ani lo yode'a" (m) / "ani lo yoda'at" (f); casual speech drops "ani": "lo yode'a".
- English present-continuous errors: Hebrew has one present tense: "ani ochel" = I eat / I'm eating.

## 6. Past and future basics

Past (ledaber, to speak):

| Person | Form | Transliteration |
|---|---|---|
| I | דיברתי | di-BAR-ti |
| you m / you f | דיברת / דיברת | di-BAR-ta / di-BART |
| he / she | דיבר / דיברה | di-BER / dib-RA |
| we | דיברנו | di-BAR-nu |
| you pl | דיברתם | dibar-TEM (f pl: dibar-TEN) |
| they | דיברו | dib-RU |

Future (ledaber): adaber, tedaber (m) / tedabri (f), yedaber (he), tedaber (she), nedaber, tedabru, yedabru.

- "Haya" (was) and "yihye" (will be): haya li zman (I had time), yihye be-seder (it will be fine).
- In speech Israelis use the near future "ani holekh + infinitive" ("ani holekh la'asot") and the infinitive after rotze/yakhol/tzarikh instead of full future forms ("ani rotze lalekhet").
- The "imperative" is usually the future: "tishlach li" (send me) m, "tishlechi li" f.
- Common slip: present tense with a past time word ("etmol ani holekh"); correct: "etmol halakhti" (I went yesterday).

## 7. Binyanim (verb patterns), practical level

Each verb has a root (shoresh, usually 3 consonants) and a pattern (binyan). Learn each verb with its pattern and meaning set (do not teach binyan names to levels 0-1).

| Binyan | Rough meaning | Present examples |
|---|---|---|
| pa'al (simple) | basic action | kotev (writes), ochel (eats), holekh (goes) |
| nif'al | passive/reflexive | nikhnas (enters), nifgash (meets, gets together) |
| pi'el | intensive/different | medaber (speaks), mesaper (tells), melamed (teaches) |
| pu'al | passive of pi'el | rare in speech, mostly participles |
| hif'il | causative | maskim (agrees), makhnis (brings in), mazmin (invites) |
| huf'al | passive of hif'il | rare in speech |
| hitpa'el | reflexive/reciprocal | mitkasher (calls someone), mitlabesh (gets dressed), mistakel (looks) |

- Present forms of pi'el start with me-, hif'il with ma-, hitpa'el with mit-: if the learner's present form starts with the wrong prefix, check the binyan.
- Useful resource for tables: Pealim (see `resources.md`).

## 8. Construct state (smichut)

Two nouns joined: the first is "shortened", the **second takes ha-** for the whole phrase to be definite.

- beit sefer (a school) / beit ha-sefer (the school).
- delet ha-bayit (the door of the house).
- Feminine -a becomes -at: makhonat kvisa (washing machine), tachanat otobus (bus stop).
- Plural: batei sefer (schools).
- In speech Israelis often prefer **shel**: ha-dira shel Dan (Dan's apartment).
- Typical error: "ha-beit ha-sefer" (article on both nouns).

## 9. Prepositions with pronoun endings

| | le- (to/for) | im (with) | shel (of/my) | et (object) | al (on/about) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I/me | li | iti | sheli | oti | alai |
| you m | lekha | itkha | shelkha | otkha | alekha |
| you f | lakh | itakh | shelakh | otakh | alaikh |
| he/him | lo | ito | shelo | oto | alav |
| she/her | la | ita | shela | ota | aleha |
| we/us | lanu | itanu | shelanu | otanu | aleinu |
| you pl | lakhem | itkhem | shelakhem | otkhem | aleikhem |
| they/them | lahem | itam | shelahem | otam | aleihem |

- Spoken Hebrew mostly uses masculine plural endings for mixed groups, and often for all-female groups too (lahen, itan, shelahen exist in careful speech).
- "Ba" (in), "mi" (from), "be-shvil" (for) also take pronoun endings; teach them as they appear.
- Whether a verb takes le- or et must be learned with the verb: "ani ro'e oto" (I see him), "ani ohev oto" (I like him), but "ani omer lo" (I tell him), "ani mesaper lo" (I tell him a story). Do not mark this as an error at levels 0-2; just model it.

## 10. Negation

- **lo** before verbs and adjectives: ani lo rotze, ze lo tov.
- **ein** = "there isn't / I don't have": ein li zman (I have no time). Never "lo yesh".
- Past of yesh/ein: haya li / lo haya li; future: yihye li / lo yihye li.
- Prohibition: **al** + future: al tid'ag (don't worry, m) / al tid'agi (f).
- **bli** = without: bli sukar. **klum / shum davar** = nothing; double negatives are normal: "ani lo rotze klum".
- "Lo" can also answer questions: "lo" (no), "ken" (yes).

## 11. Possession and "yesh li"

- "I have X" = **yesh li X**: yesh li ach (I have a brother). Past: haya li; future: yihye li. Negative: ein li.
- "My X" = **ha-X sheli** (ha-sefer sheli). With family terms the article is optional: ima sheli, ha-ima sheli.
- Do not use "yesh li" for "I am" statements ("I am hungry" = "ani ra-EV (m) / re-E-va (f)", not "yesh li ra'av").
- Hebrew has no verb "to have"; the dative pattern also covers feelings: "kar li" (I'm cold), "ba li" (I feel like), "ko'ev li ha-rosh".

## 12. Questions

- Yes/no questions: rising intonation, same word order: "ata gar be-Tel Aviv?" Formal: **ha'im** at the start ("ha'im ata gar ...?").
- Question words: ma (what), mi (who), eifo (where), me'eifo (from where), le'an (where to), matai (when), lama (why), eikh (how), kama (how much/many), eize (which, m) / eizo (which, f).
- Tag: "nakhon?" ("right?").
- Answer patterns: "ken" / "lo", or repeat the verb: "yesh?" "yesh."

## 13. Numbers and gendered counting

Counting nouns uses gendered numbers; the "-a" form goes with masculine nouns, the short form with feminine nouns (counterintuitive for English speakers).

| | m (with masculine nouns) | f (with feminine nouns, and for counting without a noun) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | echad | achat |
| 2 | shnayim (before noun: shnei) | shtayim (shtei) |
| 3 | shlosha | shalosh |
| 4 | arba'a | arba |
| 5 | chamisha | chamesh |
| 6 | shisha | shesh |
| 7 | shiv'a | sheva |
| 8 | shmona | shmone |
| 9 | tish'a | tesha |
| 10 | asara | eser |

- 11 = achad-asar (m) / achat-esre (f); 12 = shneim-asar (m) / shteim-esre (f).
- Tens are the same for both genders: esrim (20), shloshim (30), arba'im (40), chamishim (50), shishim (60), shiv'im (70), shmonim (80), tish'im (90). me'a (100), matayim (200), elef (1000).
- Examples: shlosha yeladim (3 boys/kids), shalosh yeladot (3 girls), shalosh sha'ot (3 hours; sha'a is feminine), shlosha chodashim (3 months).
- 1 and 2: yeled echad / yalda achat (number after the noun), shnei yeladim / shtei yeladot.
- Without a noun (phone numbers, bus numbers, prices, counting), use the feminine form: achat, shtayim, shalosh. Time: "ha-sha'a shalosh" (it's three o'clock), "shalosh va-chetzi" (3:30).
- In speech Israelis often drop the gender distinction in 3-10 ("shalosh yeladim"). Teach the standard form, but do not mark the casual form as an error at levels 0-2.

## 14. How Israelis simplify in speech

- Drop "ani" in the present when clear: "lo yode'a", "rotze kafe?"
- Use "efshar", "yesh", "tzarikh", "bo nelekh" instead of full conjugations.
- "Shel" instead of smichut; masculine plural for mixed groups; infinitive after modal words.
- Short, direct sentences; interjections: "nu", "yalla", "tov", "stam", "bekitzur". See `speech-and-slang.md` for register.

## 15. Fast explanations for common errors

| Error | One-line explanation |
|---|---|
| ani rotze (woman) | Present verbs change with the speaker's gender: rotza. |
| yesh lekha (to a shop) | "lekha" is to one man; to a business say "lakhem". |
| ani cham (I'm hot) | Weather feelings use the "li" pattern: cham li. |
| ha-beit ha-sefer | In smichut only the second noun takes ha-: beit ha-sefer. |
| lo yesh li | Negative of yesh is ein: ein li. |
| liskor / lehaskir | liskor = rent as tenant; lehaskir = rent out. |
| et missing | Definite object takes et: ani ro'e et ha-bayit. |
| two brothers = shnayim achim | Before a noun use shnei: shnei achim. |
| "he" for a woman in a story | Past/future need he/she endings: halkha, katva. |
