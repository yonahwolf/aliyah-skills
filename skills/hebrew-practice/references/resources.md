## Ulpan (structured Hebrew classes)

**Ministry of Aliyah and Integration (Misrad HaAliyah VeHaKlita) subsidized ulpan for new olim**
- Per a Nefesh B'Nefesh benefits page: a standard subsidized course lasts about 5 months, 5 hours a day, 5 days a week; the benefit can be used up to 18 months after aliyah; the location depends on the address on the Teudat Zehut (ID). That page did not name the formal criteria.
- **Not verified on gov.il:** the Ministry's own service pages (eligibility check and the personal account for new olim) appear in search results but could not be loaded in this session. Tell the learner to confirm hours, eligibility, deadlines, and how to register with the Ministry (gov.il), Nefesh B'Nefesh, or the local absorption office.
- Eligibility depends on olim status (for example, returning residents and others may differ). Do not state eligibility as fact; send the learner to the official check.

**Ulpan Etzion (Jewish Agency for Israel)**
- Verified from the Jewish Agency's page: a five-month Hebrew immersion program for new immigrants, running since 1949, with Hebrew study, job preparation for employment in Israel or for the IDF, and cultural and social activities.
- Eligibility on the page: college graduates aged 22-35, including singles and couples without children. Jerusalem campus (Beit Canada Absorption Center, Armon Hanatziv). Starts in January and July. The page listed a fee (NIS 7,400 for five months, plus a deposit) and a lower price for external participants; fees and terms change, so verify.

**Municipal ulpanim**
- Verified from the Tel Aviv-Yafo municipality: two municipal ulpanim (Gordon and Neveh Zedek), classes five days a week, mornings and a typical evening track; a full course is about 25 hours a week; advanced and conversation courses exist. The sample schedule on the page is from 2019 and likely outdated, so check with the ulpan.
- Other cities have their own ulpanim; ask the local municipality (Misrad HaKlita office or city hall).

**University Hebrew courses**
- Universities run Hebrew courses (for example, a summer ulpan at the Hebrew University's Rothberg International School appears on its site). Reichman University students should ask their program office about Hebrew support. Details not verified; confirm with the institution.

## Dictionaries and grammar tools

- **Pealim (pealim.com):** verified. Hebrew verb conjugation tables with transcriptions close to modern pronunciation, search by inflected form, dictionary, display options for vowelled/unvowelled text and transliteration. A reference tool, not a textbook; it recommends learning the alphabet and taking a course.
- **Morfix:** Hebrew-English dictionary and translator (listed on Pealim's links page as free, with paid extras such as a nikud tool). Practical for everyday words.
- **Milog:** Hebrew-Hebrew dictionary listing all word forms (listed on Pealim's links page). Useful for level 3-4.
- **Reverso (dictionary.reverso.net, Hebrew-English):** verified that a Hebrew-English dictionary page exists; useful for examples in context.
- **Wiktionary (Hebrew):** translations, usage examples, etymology (coverage of common words incomplete per Pealim).
- **Google Translate:** helpful for rare words; check gender and register, because machine translation often defaults to the masculine form.
- **Eliezer Ben-Yehuda dictionary:** verified as a historical Hebrew dictionary (five volumes published by Eliezer Ben-Yehuda in his lifetime and twelve more later; digitized by volunteers, available via Sefaria). It documents the revival of Hebrew and is excellent for history and etymology, but it is not a practical tool for modern everyday learning. For everyday work, use Morfix, Milog, and Pealim.

## Hebrew news and listening at the learner's level

- **Ivrit (Jerusalem Post, jpost.com/ivrit):** verified. Israel and Middle East news with vowel marks, written for readers with basic Hebrew who want to improve.
- **Ministry of Education news archive for Hebrew learners ("Yedi'ot Kodmot" on the pedagogical portal):** verified. Short, fully vowelized news items on Israel and the world, holidays, science, and sports, each with a glossary of harder words.
- **Kan (the public broadcaster) radio and TV news (kan.org.il):** Kan has the radio station Kan Bet (Reshet Bet) and news programs. These are regular-speed Hebrew news, not simplified; use for advanced listening, not for beginners.
- **"Hadashot BeSafa Kala" (news in easy language):** **could not be verified** as a current program or site in this session; do not recommend it by that name unless the learner finds it.
- **Galei Tzahal (Army Radio):** not verified as easy-Hebrew; regular-speed station.
- For beginners: children's songs, subtitled Israeli series (Hebrew subtitles), podcasts for learners, WhatsApp voice notes from friends, and signs and menus in your neighborhood.

## Human practice

- A tutor or a language-exchange partner for live feedback on pronunciation, stress, and spontaneous speech.
- A native speaker to confirm important texts (legal, official, medical, contracts, visas, bank forms). Claude can draft and explain, not certify.
- Recording yourself saying the same sentence from a session and comparing it with native audio.
