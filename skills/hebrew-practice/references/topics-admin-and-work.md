# Topics: admin, work, and study (7-13)

Conventions: see `topic-bank.md`. m = masculine, f = feminine, pl = plural. Transliteration: ח=ch, כ=kh, צ=tz; in tables, hyphens split syllables and CAPS marks the stressed syllable.

For official or legal texts (leases, bank forms, medical or army paperwork), Claude may help practice and draft but the learner should have a native speaker or professional confirm the final wording.

## 7. Apartment and landlords

**Prompts**
- L1: Ask a landlord how many rooms the apartment has and how much the rent is.
- L2: Text a landlord that there is a leak in the kitchen and ask if someone can come tomorrow.
- L3: You are viewing an apartment. Ask about arnona, the building committee fee, whether there is air conditioning and a safe room (mamad), and when you could move in.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| אני מחפש דירה להשכרה / אני מחפשת דירה להשכרה | ani me-cha-PES (m) / me-cha-PE-set (f) di-RA le-has-ka-RA | I'm looking for an apartment to rent |
| בעל הבית / בעלת הבית | BA-al ha-BA-yit (m) / BA-a-lat ha-BA-yit (f) | landlord / landlady |
| שוכר / שוכרת | so-KHER (m) / so-KHE-ret (f) | tenant |
| שכר דירה | se-KHAR di-RA | rent (the amount) |
| חוזה | CHO-ze | contract |
| שיקים | she-KIM | checks (post-dated checks are sometimes requested) |
| כמה חדרים? | ka-MA cha-da-RIM? | how many rooms? |
| ארנונה | ar-NO-na | municipal tax |
| ועד בית | va-AD BA-yit | building committee (and its fee) |
| חשבונות | chesh-bo-NOT | utility bills |
| חשמל / מים / גז | chash-MAL / MA-yim / GAZ | electricity / water / gas |
| יש מזגן? | yesh maz-GAN? | is there air conditioning? |
| מרפסת / מעלית | mir-PE-set / ma-a-LIT | balcony / elevator |
| ממ"ד | ma-MAD | safe room (merchav mugan dirati) |
| מתי אפשר להיכנס? | ma-TAI ef-SHAR le-hi-ka-NES? | when can I move in? |
| יש נזילה במטבח | yesh ne-zi-LA ba-mit-BACH | there's a leak in the kitchen |
| הדוד מקולקל | ha-DUD me-kul-KAL | the water heater is broken |
| אפשר שמישהו יבוא לבדוק מחר? | ef-SHAR she-ma-SHE-hu ya-VO liv-DOK ma-CHAR? | can someone come check tomorrow? |

Sample WhatsApp (neutral register):

שלום דוד, מדברת דנה מהדירה ברחוב הרצל. יש נזילה בכיור במטבח. אפשר שמישהו יבוא לבדוק מחר? תודה.

shalom David, me-da-BE-ret Dana me-ha-di-RA be-re-CHOV HER-tzel. yesh ne-zi-LA ba-ki-YOR ba-mit-BACH. ef-SHAR she-ma-SHE-hu ya-VO liv-DOK ma-CHAR? to-DA.

**Common mistakes**
- **liskor vs lehaskir:** to rent as a tenant is "lis-KOR" (לשכור); to rent out is "le-has-KIR" (להשכיר). Swapping them is very common.
- Room count: Israelis count the living room as a room ("3 rooms" often means two bedrooms plus a living room; "3.5" includes a small extra room). Ask to confirm what is meant.
- Gender: "ani shokher" vs "shokheret"; "ba'al ha-bayit" is male, "ba'alat ha-bayit" is female.
- Over-formal letter-style openings in WhatsApp.

**Culture**
- Landlord communication is mostly WhatsApp and phone; short, direct messages are normal.
- Many leases ask for guarantees (checks, guarantors, or deposits); terms vary. Have a trusted Hebrew reader or professional review the contract.
- Building committee (va'ad bayit) fees are separate from rent, as are arnona and utilities, unless the contract says otherwise.

## 8. Bank, post office, Kupat Cholim, pharmacy

**Prompts**
- L1: Tell the pharmacist you have a headache and ask for something for it.
- L2: Call your health fund to book an appointment with a family doctor next week.
- L3: At the bank, say you want to open an account, ask which documents you need, and ask whether you can use the app.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| אני רוצה לפתוח חשבון | ani ro-TZE / ro-TZA lif-TO-ach chesh-BON | I want to open an account |
| תעודת זהות / דרכון | te-u-DAT ze-HUT / dar-KON | ID card / passport |
| העברה בנקאית | ha-a-va-RA ban-ka-IT | bank transfer |
| כרטיס אשראי | kar-TIS ash-RAI | credit card |
| אפשר לקבוע תור? | ef-SHAR lik-BO-a TOR? | can I book an appointment? |
| קופת חולים | KU-pat cho-LIM | health fund (the four funds are Clalit, Maccabi, Meuhedet, Leumit) |
| רופא משפחה | ro-FE mish-pa-CHA | family doctor |
| מרשם | mir-SHAM | prescription |
| בית מרקחת | beit mir-KA-chat | pharmacy |
| אני צריך משהו נגד כאב ראש | ani tza-RIKH (m) / tzri-KHA (f) ma-SHE-hu NE-ged ke-EV rosh | I need something for a headache |
| כואב לי הראש / כואבת לי הבטן | ko-EV li ha-ROSH / ko-E-vet li ha-BE-ten | my head hurts / my stomach hurts |
| כואב לי הגרון | ko-EV li ha-ga-RON | my throat hurts |
| יש לי חום | yesh li CHOM | I have a fever |
| אני חולה | ani cho-LE (m) / cho-LA (f) | I'm sick (spelled the same for both genders) |
| דואר / חבילה | DO-ar / cha-vi-LA | post office / package |
| אני רוצה לשלוח מכתב | ani ro-TZE / ro-TZA lish-LO-ach mikh-TAV | I want to send a letter |

**Common mistakes**
- "Ko'ev li" agrees with the body part: head and throat are masculine (ko'ev), stomach is feminine (ko'evet). Not "ani ko'ev" ("I hurt").
- "Ani chole/chola" for "I'm sick"; do not say "yesh li sick."
- "Efshar li ...?" instead of the simple "efshar ...?"
- Forgetting "et" and definite article in "et ha-teudat zehut".

**Culture**
- Appointments and service often run through apps and phone lines; hold times and queue numbers are normal.
- Pharmacies sell many things over the counter; ask the pharmacist (ha-ro-KE-ach / ha-ro-KE-chet, the pharmacist m/f) in simple words.
- New olim should confirm health-fund and bank procedures with the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration, Nefesh B'Nefesh, or the institution itself; rules change.

## 9. Work and meetings

**Prompts**
- L1: Introduce yourself to a new team and say what your job is.
- L2: Ask a colleague when they have time to meet this week and agree on a time.
- L3: In a meeting, politely say you disagree with the plan, give one reason, and suggest an alternative; end by saying you'll send a summary by email.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| אני עובד ב... / אני עובדת ב... | ani o-VED (m) / o-VE-det (f) be ... | I work at ... |
| אני מהנדס / מהנדסת | ani me-han-DES (m) / me-han-DE-set (f) | I'm an engineer |
| פגישה | pgi-SHA | meeting (appointment) |
| אפשר לקבוע פגישה? | ef-SHAR lik-BO-a pgi-SHA? | can we schedule a meeting? |
| מתי נוח לך? | ma-TAI NO-ach LE-kha (m) / lakh (f)? | when is convenient for you? |
| בוא נקבע / בואי נקבע | BO nik-BA (m) / bo-I nik-BA (f) | let's schedule |
| יש לי שאלה | yesh li she-e-LA | I have a question |
| אני לא בטוח / בטוחה | ani lo ba-TU-ach (m) / be-tu-CHA (f) | I'm not sure |
| אני אשלח מייל | ani esh-LACH meil | I'll send an email |
| אני בודק ואחזור אליך | ani bo-DEK (m) / bo-DE-ket (f) ve-ech-ZOR e-LE-kha / e-LAIKH | I'm checking and I'll get back to you |
| אפשר לשתף מסך? | ef-SHAR le-sha-TEF ma-SAKH? | can I share my screen? |
| מועד אחרון | MO-ed a-cha-RON | deadline |
| משימה / צוות | me-si-MA / TZE-vet | task / team |
| מנהל / מנהלת | me-na-HEL / me-na-HE-let | manager (m / f) |
| ראיון עבודה / קורות חיים | re-a-YON a-vo-DA / ko-ROT cha-YIM | job interview / CV |

**Common mistakes**
- Missing preposition: "oved be-chevra", not "oved chevra".
- Gender in job titles and present-tense verbs.
- Tone when disagreeing: "ani lo mas-KIM" (m) / "ani lo mas-KI-ma" (f), אני לא מסכים / מסכימה, means "I don't agree" and is acceptable in meetings; a softer opener is "ani cho-SHEV (m) / cho-SHE-vet (f) she ..." (אני חושב / חושבת ש..., "I think that ...").
- Word-for-word "make a meeting" ("la'asot pgisha"); use "likbo'a pgisha".

**Culture**
- Flat hierarchies and first names are the norm; disagreeing openly in meetings is usually normal debate, not hostility.
- The Israeli work week is typically Sunday to Thursday (Friday often short or off); check your employer.
- Tech and business Hebrew mixes in many English words ("deadline", "feedback"); use them if the learner is stuck, then give the Hebrew.
- WhatsApp groups are common for team coordination.

## 10. University

**Prompts**
- L1: Ask a classmate which course they are taking and when the exam is.
- L2: Email or message a lecturer asking for an extension on an assignment.
- L3: Visit the registrar (mazkirut): ask about registering for a course, a form you need, and the deadline; ask them to repeat slowly.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| קורס / סמסטר | KURS / se-MES-ter | course / semester |
| מרצה | mar-TZE (m) / mar-TZA (f) | lecturer |
| תרגיל | tar-GIL | exercise/tutorial |
| מבחן | miv-CHAN | exam |
| ציון | tzi-YUN | grade |
| מועד א' / מועד ב' | MO-ed A-lef / MO-ed bet | first / second exam date |
| מטלה | ma-ta-LA | assignment |
| מזכירות | maz-ki-RUT | administrative office |
| תואר ראשון / תואר שני | TO-ar ri-SHON / TO-ar she-NI | bachelor's / master's degree |
| שכר לימוד | se-KHAR li-MUD | tuition |
| מעונות | ma-o-NOT | dormitories |
| אני לא הבנתי את החומר | ani lo he-VAN-ti et ha-CHO-mer | I didn't understand the material |
| אפשר הארכה? | ef-SHAR ha-a-ra-KHA? | could I have an extension? |
| אפשר להגיש באיחור? | ef-SHAR le-ha-GISH be-i-CHUR? | may I submit late? |
| אני לומד למבחן | ani lo-MED la-miv-CHAN | I'm studying for the exam |
| מילואים | mi-lu-IM | reserve duty |

**Common mistakes**
- "Lilmod le-mivchan" (study for an exam) not "lilmod al"; "lehagish matala" (submit an assignment).
- Gender on "ani lomed/lomedet".
- Starting a message to a lecturer with a letter-style "Dear Professor ... Yours sincerely"; a short polite message is normal: "shalom, [name]," then the request.
- Confusing "miv-CHAN" (exam) with "bchina" (also exam/test, a bit more formal): both are understood.

**Culture**
- First names with lecturers are common; asking questions in class and challenging ideas is normal.
- Classmates often include people who completed army service and are older; reserve duty (miluim) can mean absences, and accommodations are usually discussed with the lecturer.
- Course groups run on WhatsApp; deadlines and announcements often appear there as well as on the university system.
- Some programs (including programs for English speakers) run in English; Hebrew practice still helps with administration and daily life.

## 11. Customer service calls

**Prompts**
- L1: Call and say you have a problem with your bill and ask for a representative.
- L2: Tell an internet company that your connection has been down for two days and ask when someone can come.
- L3: You were charged twice. Explain, ask for a refund, ask for a reference number, and politely ask to be called back.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| שלום, אני מתקשר לגבי ... / אני מתקשרת לגבי ... | shalom, ani mit-ka-SHER (m) / mit-ka-SHE-ret (f) li-GVEI ... | hello, I'm calling about ... |
| יש לי בעיה עם החשבון | yesh li be-a-YA im ha-chesh-BON | I have a problem with my account/bill |
| חויבתי פעמיים | chu-YAV-ti pa-a-MA-yim | I was charged twice |
| אפשר לדבר עם נציג? | ef-SHAR le-da-BER im na-TZIG? | can I speak with a representative? (f rep: ne-tzi-GA) |
| מה מספר הפנייה? | ma mis-PAR hap-ni-YA? | what's the reference number? |
| אני מחכה כבר חצי שעה | ani me-cha-KE (m) / me-cha-KA (f) kvar CHE-tzi sha-A | I've been waiting half an hour |
| תוכל לחזור אליי? (m) / תוכלי לחזור אליי? (f) | tu-KHAL / tukh-LI la-cha-ZOR e-LAI? | could you call me back? |
| אני רוצה לבטל מנוי | ani ro-TZE / ro-TZA le-va-TEL mi-NUY | I want to cancel a subscription |
| החזר כספי | ha-CHZER kas-PI | refund |
| תודה על העזרה | to-DA al ha-ez-RA | thanks for your help |

**Common mistakes**
- "Ani mitkasher / mitkasheret" needs the gender; "li-gvei" (about, regarding) is the safe, natural choice.
- Literal translation of "I would like to complain"; say the problem plainly ("yesh li be'aya ...").
- Passive voice: "chuyavti" (I was charged) is a passive/pual form; learners often say "hem chiyvu oti" (they charged me), which is also fine and simpler.

**Culture**
- Phone menus offer language choices; learners may need to press a key for English or stay on Hebrew. Do not be embarrassed to say "efshar be-anglit?" (can we do this in English?).
- Persistence is normal: calling back or asking for a manager ("ef-SHAR le-da-BER im me-na-HEL?", אפשר לדבר עם מנהל?).
- Writing down the name of the representative and the reference number is practical in any language.

## 12. Emergencies and health

**Prompts** (keep calm, factual, practical; never frighten the learner)
- L1: Say you feel sick and need a doctor.
- L2: Describe a minor accident to a bystander and ask them to call an ambulance.
- L3: Explain to a doctor your symptoms (fever, sore throat, two days), that you are allergic to penicillin, and ask for a prescription.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| עזרה! | ez-RA! | help! |
| תתקשרו למשטרה | tit-kash-RU la-mish-ta-RA | call the police (pl; to a group or a polite request) |
| תתקשרו לאמבולנס | tit-kash-RU la-am-BU-lans | call an ambulance |
| אני צריך רופא / אני צריכה רופא | ani tza-RIKH (m) / tzri-KHA (f) ro-FE | I need a doctor |
| יש תאונה | yesh te-u-NA | there's an accident |
| יש שריפה | yesh sre-FA | there's a fire |
| אני לא מרגיש טוב / אני לא מרגישה טוב | ani lo mar-GISH (m) / mar-gi-SHA (f) tov | I don't feel well |
| אני אלרגי ל... / אני אלרגית ל... | ani a-LER-gi (m) / a-ler-GIT (f) le ... | I'm allergic to ... |
| חדר מיון | CHE-der mi-YUN | emergency room |
| מגן דוד אדום | ma-GEN da-VID a-DOM | Magen David Adom (ambulance service) |
| אזעקה | az-a-KA | siren |
| מקלט / ממ"ד | mik-LAT / ma-MAD | public shelter / apartment safe room |
| לרדת למקלט | la-RE-det la-mik-LAT | go down to the shelter |

Emergency numbers (commonly cited; confirm and save them in your phone): police 100, Magen David Adom ambulance 101, fire 102.

**Common mistakes**
- Using "ani ko'ev" for "I'm in pain"; say what hurts ("ko'ev li ha-rosh") or "yesh li ke'ev".
- Gender on "tzarikh/tzrikha", "margish/margisha", "alergi/alergit".
- Forgetting the word for the symptom; point or say it in English and ask "ma ze be-ivrit?" (what is this in Hebrew?).

**Culture**
- Israelis are direct and practical in emergencies. Short, clear sentences are better than perfect grammar.
- Many people carry the national emergency numbers and shelter/alert apps on their phone; follow official Home Front Command guidance (Pikud Ha'oref) for sirens and shelter, and learn the instructions from official sources, not from a language lesson.
- Medical and legal communication: have a native speaker or professional translator confirm important details.

## 13. Army and lone-soldier basics

Practice here stays general and non-operational. Eligibility, rights, and procedures: go to the IDF, the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration, and official lone-soldier organizations; do not rely on this skill for them.

**Prompts**
- L1: Introduce yourself to a fellow soldier or recruit and say where you are from.
- L2: Ask your commander if you can go home for the weekend; say you are a lone soldier.
- L3: Explain to a friend (by message) what a typical day during basic training is like and what you miss from home.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| צבא / צה"ל | tza-VA / tza-HAL | army / the IDF |
| חייל בודד / חיילת בודדה | cha-YAL bo-DED (m) / cha-YE-let bdu-DA (f) | lone soldier |
| גיוס | gi-YUS | enlistment |
| טירונות | ti-ro-NUT | basic training |
| מילואים | mi-lu-IM | reserve duty |
| מפקד / מפקדת | me-fa-KED / me-fa-KE-det | commander (m / f) |
| בסיס | BA-sis | base |
| אני יוצא הביתה / אני יוצאת הביתה | ani yo-TZE (m) / yo-TZE-et (f) ha-BAI-ta | I'm going home (on leave) |
| שמירה | shmi-RA | guard duty |
| אישור | i-SHUR | approval |
| שחרור | shich-RUR | discharge |
| אחי | a-CHI | bro (casual, to a man) |

**Common mistakes**
- Mistaking army slang for formal Hebrew; many terms are acronyms or nicknames that change over time.
- Gender in titles ("mefaked/mefakedet", "chayal/chayelet").
- Forgetting the article: "ani ba-tzava" (I'm in the army), not "ani be-tzava".

**Culture**
- Army service shapes Israeli social life and small talk (where did you serve, what unit); "ma ha-taf-KID shel-KHA?" (m) (what is/was your role?) is a normal question. Respect that some topics are private.
- Hebrew in the army mixes slang, abbreviations, and English words; learners should expect to ask "ma ze omer?" (what does it mean?).
