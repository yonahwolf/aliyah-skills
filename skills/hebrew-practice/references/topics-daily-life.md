# Topics: daily life (1-6)

Conventions: see `topic-bank.md`. m = masculine, f = feminine, pl = plural. Transliteration: ח=ch, כ=kh, צ=tz; in tables, hyphens split syllables and CAPS marks the stressed syllable (see `pronunciation-and-transliteration.md`).

---

## 1. Greetings and introductions

**Prompts**
- L1: You meet an Israeli at a party. Say hello, say your name, and say you are from the US.
- L2: Introduce yourself to a new coworker: your name, where you live, that you are learning Hebrew, and ask how long they have lived here.
- L3: A neighbor invites you in and asks about you. Explain why you moved, what you do, and apologize that your Hebrew is still basic.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| שלום | shalom | hello / goodbye / peace (neutral) |
| היי | hai | hi (casual) |
| בוקר טוב | BO-ker tov | good morning |
| ערב טוב | E-rev tov | good evening |
| לילה טוב | LAI-la tov | good night |
| מה שלומך? | ma shlomkha? (m) / ma shlomekh? (f) | how are you? |
| מה נשמע? | ma nish-MA? | what's up? / how are you? (casual, universal) |
| הכול בסדר | ha-KOL be-SE-der | everything's fine |
| מצוין / לא רע | me-tzu-YAN / lo RA | excellent / not bad |
| נעים להכיר | na-IM le-ha-KIR | nice to meet you |
| קוראים לי ... | ko-RIM li ... | my name is ... (lit. "they call me") |
| אני מארצות הברית | a-NI me-ar-TZOT ha-BRIT | I'm from the United States |
| אני לומד עברית / אני לומדת עברית | ani lo-MED ivrit (m) / ani lo-ME-det ivrit (f) | I'm learning Hebrew |
| אני מדבר קצת עברית / אני מדברת קצת עברית | ani me-da-BER ktzat iv-RIT (m) / ani me-da-BE-ret ktzat iv-RIT (f) | I speak a little Hebrew |
| אפשר לדבר יותר לאט? | ef-SHAR le-da-BER yo-TER le-AT? | could you speak more slowly? |
| לא הבנתי | lo he-VAN-ti | I didn't understand |
| אפשר לחזור על זה? | ef-SHAR la-cha-ZOR al ze? | could you repeat that? |
| להתראות | le-hit-ra-OT | see you / goodbye |
| שיהיה לך יום טוב | she-yih-YE LE-kha (m) / she-yih-YE lakh (f) yom tov | have a good day |

**Common mistakes**
- Gender on "I" verbs and adjectives (see `grammar-guide.md`): a woman says "ani lomedet", not "lomed".
- Using "ma shlomkha" to a woman or "ma shlomekh" to a man.
- Translating "I am called" literally; Israelis say "korim li" or just "ani Dana".
- Adding "ani" plus a conjugated "to be": Hebrew has no present "am/is/are" ("ani student", not "ani hu student").
- English-style "How do you do?" / over-formal openings; Israelis say "ma nishma?" to almost everyone.

**Culture**
- First names with everyone, including bosses and professors. Hebrew has no formal "you" like vous or Sie.
- "Ma nishma?" gets a short answer ("sababa", "be-seder", "tov") or a real conversation; both are fine.
- Direct personal questions early (where you are from, do you have kids, what do you earn) are usually curiosity, not rudeness.
- Handshakes and, among friends, cheek kisses or hugs are common; follow the other person's lead.

## 2. Cafés and restaurants

**Prompts**
- L1: Order a coffee with milk and ask for the check.
- L2: Order a latte and a croissant, ask if they have oat milk, and say you will sit inside.
- L3: Your dish arrived without what you ordered and you are vegetarian. Tell the waiter politely, ask for it to be fixed, and ask for the check.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| אפשר קפה הפוך? | ef-SHAR ka-FE ha-FUKH? | could I have a latte? (kafe hafukh = Israeli latte) |
| אני רוצה קפה, בבקשה | a-NI ro-TZE (m) / ro-TZA (f) ka-FE, be-va-ka-SHA | I'd like a coffee, please |
| קפה שחור / אספרסו / נס | ka-FE sha-CHOR / es-PRE-so / NES | black coffee / espresso / instant coffee |
| עם חלב / בלי סוכר | im cha-LAV / bli su-KAR | with milk / without sugar |
| יש לכם חלב שיבולת שועל? | yesh la-KHEM cha-LAV shi-BO-let shu-AL? | do you have oat milk? |
| חלב סויה / חלב שקדים | cha-LAV SO-ya / cha-LAV she-ka-DIM | soy milk / almond milk |
| אפשר תפריט? | ef-SHAR taf-RIT? | could I have a menu? |
| שולחן לשניים, בבקשה | shul-CHAN lish-NA-yim, be-va-ka-SHA | a table for two, please |
| לשבת או לקחת? | la-SHE-vet o la-KA-chat? | for here or to go? |
| אני אקח ... | a-NI e-KACH ... | I'll take ... |
| בלי בצל, בבקשה | bli ba-TZAL, be-va-ka-SHA | no onion, please |
| אני צמחוני / צמחונית | ani tzim-cho-NI (m) / tzim-cho-NIT (f) | I'm vegetarian |
| אני טבעוני / טבעונית | ani tiv-o-NI (m) / tiv-o-NIT (f) | I'm vegan |
| יש לי אלרגיה לאגוזים | yesh li a-LER-gya la-e-go-ZIM | I'm allergic to nuts |
| מים, בבקשה | MA-yim, be-va-ka-SHA | water, please |
| אפשר חשבון? | ef-SHAR chesh-BON? | could I have the check? |
| אפשר לשלם בכרטיס? | ef-SHAR le-sha-LEM be-kar-TIS? | can I pay by card? |
| הכול היה טעים | ha-KOL ha-YA ta-IM | everything was tasty |
| בתיאבון | be-te-a-VON | bon appétit |

**Common mistakes**
- "Ani rotze" with the wrong gender (spelled the same, pronounced differently).
- Adding "li" to "efshar" ("efshar li ...?" is understood but unnatural; plain "efshar ...?").
- Using "yesh lekha" (to one man) for the staff; use plural "yesh lakhem" for a business.
- Saying "ani tzarikh/tzrikha" ("I need") for ordering; it sounds like a demand. Use "efshar" or "ani rotze/rotza".
- Mixing up "tov" (good/ok) and "ta'im" (tasty).

**Culture**
- Staff are casual and direct; you may be addressed with "ma tirtzu?" (what would you like, pl). Do not expect long formalities.
- Tipping in sit-down restaurants is customary (commonly around 10-15%); check whether service is already added. Confirm current norms locally.
- Many places bring salatim (small salads) and bread to the table.
- Asking for the check is often done with a gesture or "cheshbon, bevakasha."

## 3. Shopping and the shuk

**Prompts**
- L1: At a market stall, ask the price of tomatoes and buy one kilo.
- L2: In a clothing store, say you are just looking, ask to try a shirt in a bigger size, and ask for the price.
- L3: At the shuk the price seems high. Ask politely for a lower price, decide what to buy, and ask for a bag and a receipt.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| כמה זה עולה? / כמה זה? | ka-MA ze o-LE? / ka-MA ze? | how much is it? |
| זה יקר / זה זול | ze ya-KAR / ze ZOL | it's expensive / it's cheap |
| יש הנחה? | yesh ha-na-CHA? | is there a discount? |
| אפשר להוריד קצת במחיר? | ef-SHAR le-ho-RID ktzat ba-ma-CHIR? | could you lower the price a bit? |
| קילו עגבניות, בבקשה | KI-lo ag-va-ni-YOT, be-va-ka-SHA | a kilo of tomatoes, please |
| חצי קילו | CHE-tzi KI-lo | half a kilo |
| אני רק מסתכל / מסתכלת | ani rak mis-ta-KEL (m) / mis-ta-KE-let (f) | I'm just looking |
| אפשר לנסות? | ef-SHAR le-na-SOT? | can I try it (on)? |
| יש לכם את זה במידה אחרת? | yesh la-KHEM et ze be-mi-DA a-CHE-ret? | do you have this in another size? |
| אני אקח את זה | a-NI e-KACH et ze | I'll take it |
| זה הכול, תודה | ze ha-KOL, to-DA | that's all, thanks |
| שקית / קבלה | sa-KIT / ka-ba-LA | bag / receipt |
| טרי | ta-RI | fresh |
| עשרים שקל | es-RIM SHE-kel | twenty shekels |

**Common mistakes**
- Leaving out "et" before a definite object ("ani ekach et ze" is right; "ani ekach ze" is a common slip).
- Counting gender: "shalosh agvaniyot" (agvaniya is feminine), "shlosha kilo" (see `grammar-guide.md`). In speech Israelis often simplify; do not over-correct at levels 0-2.
- Saying "ze yakar" to mean "it is dear to me"; it only means "expensive".
- Using "kama" only for price: it means both "how much" and "how many".

**Culture**
- Bargaining is normal in some stalls and flea markets, but not in malls or supermarkets. Ask politely and be ready to walk away.
- Shuk vendors call out prices and offers; a short "toda, lo, ani rak mistakel" is a fine way to move on.
- Prices are in shekels (shekel, plural shkalim); in speech, Israelis often drop the plural ("esrim shekel").

## 4. Transport, taxis, trains, and Rav-Kav

**Prompts**
- L1: At a bus stop, ask whether this bus goes to Tel Aviv.
- L2: Ask where to load your Rav-Kav and how much a ride to Jerusalem costs; then ask which platform the train leaves from.
- L3: You are in a taxi and the driver took a long route. Tell him your destination, ask him to use the meter, and ask how much it will be.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| איפה התחנה? | EI-fo ha-ta-cha-NA? | where is the stop/station? |
| האוטובוס הזה נוסע ל...? | ha-o-to-BUS ha-ZE no-SE-a le ...? | does this bus go to ...? |
| מתי האוטובוס הבא? | ma-TAI ha-o-to-BUS ha-BA? | when is the next bus? |
| אפשר לרדת פה? | ef-SHAR la-RE-det po? | can I get off here? |
| רב-קו | rav-KAV | Rav-Kav (public transport card) |
| אפשר לטעון רב-קו? | ef-SHAR lit-ON rav-KAV? | can I load a Rav-Kav? |
| איזה רציף? | EI-ze ra-TZIF? | which platform? |
| רכבת | ra-KE-vet | train |
| מונית / שירות | mo-NIT / she-RUT | taxi / shared minibus taxi on a fixed route |
| אפשר מונה, בבקשה? | ef-SHAR mo-NE, be-va-ka-SHA? | meter on, please? |
| כמה זה יעלה? | ka-MA ze ya-a-LE? | how much will it cost? |
| לתל אביב, בבקשה | le-tel a-VIV, be-va-ka-SHA | to Tel Aviv, please |
| נהג | na-HAG | driver |

**Common mistakes**
- "Ani rotze lalekhet" (I want to walk) when you mean "go (by transport)": use "lin-SO-a" (לנסוע, to travel/go by vehicle).
- Forgetting "et" and prepositions: "nose'a le-Yerushalayim", not "nose'a Yerushalayim".
- Asking "when is the bus?" without "ha-ba" (next): "matai ha-otobus ha-ba?"
- English word order inside questions.

**Culture**
- Many apps are used for transit and taxis (Moovit, Waze, Gett are widely used); download and use them; they are normal.
- Most public transport runs reduced or not at all from Friday afternoon to Saturday evening; check before planning. Verify current schedules.
- Drivers and fellow riders are direct; "slicha" and "toda" are welcome but not expected in long forms.
- A "sherut" is a shared taxi on a fixed route; confirm the fare before you board.

## 5. Directions

**Prompts**
- L1: Ask a passerby where the bank is.
- L2: Ask how to get to the train station and whether it is far to walk.
- L3: Explain to a friend (by message) how to reach your apartment: go straight, turn right at the traffic light, then left at the corner.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| סליחה, איפה הבנק? | sli-CHA, EI-fo ha-BANK? | excuse me, where is the bank? |
| ימינה / שמאלה / ישר | ya-MI-na / smo-LA / ya-SHAR | right / left / straight |
| תפנה ימינה (m) / תפני ימינה (f) | tif-NE ya-MI-na / tif-NI ya-MI-na | turn right |
| בפינה | be-pi-NA | on the corner |
| ברמזור | ba-ram-ZOR | at the traffic light |
| בצומת | ba-tzo-MET | at the intersection |
| קרוב / רחוק | ka-ROV / ra-CHOK | close / far |
| כמה זמן ללכת ברגל? | ka-MA za-MAN la-LE-khet be-RE-gel? | how long to walk? |
| עשר דקות | E-ser da-KOT | ten minutes |
| אני מחפש את ... / אני מחפשת את ... | ani me-cha-PES (m) / me-cha-PE-set (f) et ... | I'm looking for ... |
| אני אבוד / אבודה | ani a-VUD (m) / a-vu-DA (f) | I'm lost |

**Common mistakes**
- Dropping "et" with a definite object: "ani mechapes et ha-bank".
- Mixing up "yamin" (right, noun) and "yamina" (to the right); same for smol / smola.
- Using the wrong imperative gender ("tifne" to a woman).
- "How long is it?" translated word for word; Israelis say "kama zman ze?" or "kama rachok?"

**Culture**
- Israelis give directions confidently, sometimes wrongly; double-check with a map or a second person.
- Street signs usually include English, but small streets and neighborhood names vary.
- People often navigate by landmarks ("at the corner with the pharmacy").

## 6. Phone and WhatsApp

**Prompts**
- L1: Answer the phone, ask who is speaking, and say you will call back.
- L2: Message a friend that you are running late and ask them to send their location.
- L3: Call a clinic to ask whether a doctor is available tomorrow, and the line cuts out; ask them to repeat.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| הלו | ha-LO | hello (on the phone) |
| מי מדבר? | mi me-da-BER? | who is speaking? |
| מדבר דן / מדברת דנה | me-da-BER Dan (m) / me-da-BE-ret Dana (f) | Dan / Dana speaking |
| אפשר לדבר עם ...? | ef-SHAR le-da-BER im ...? | could I speak with ...? |
| רגע, אני אחזור אליך | RE-ga, ani ech-ZOR e-LE-kha (m) / e-LAIKH (f) | one moment, I'll get back to you |
| אני אשלח לך הודעה בוואטסאפ | ani esh-LACH le-KHA (m) / lakh (f) ho-da-A be-vats-AP | I'll send you a WhatsApp message |
| תשלח לי את המיקום (m) / תשלחי לי (f) | tish-LACH li et ha-mi-KUM / tish-le-CHI li | send me the location |
| אני לא שומע טוב / שומעת טוב | ani lo sho-ME-a (m) / sho-MA-at (f) tov | I can't hear well |
| הקו נקטע | ha-KAV nik-TA | the line cut out |
| אין בעיה | EIN be-a-YA | no problem |
| סגור | sa-GUR | agreed / deal (casual) |
| אני מאחר / מאחרת | ani me-a-CHER (m) / me-a-CHE-ret (f) | I'm running late |
| נתראה | nit-ra-E | see you (lit. "we'll be seen") |
| חחח | ha-ha-ha (written) | lol (Hebrew has no "lol"; חחח is typed laughter) |

**Common mistakes**
- Translating "Hello, this is ..." as "ze ..." with "ani": Israelis say "medaber Dan" (Dan speaking).
- Wrong gender on "shome'a/shome'at" and "me'acher/me'acheret".
- Writing long formal greetings on WhatsApp; short and casual is normal.
- Voice-note etiquette: "hoda'a kolit" (voice message); people often send many short ones.

**Culture**
- WhatsApp is the default channel for personal and often work and community communication, including landlords, class groups, and kindergartens.
- Calls often start without small talk; "ma nishma" then business is normal.
- Typing Hebrew without vowels and with typos is normal; do not worry about perfect spelling in chat.
