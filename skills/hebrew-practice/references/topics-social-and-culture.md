# Topics: social life and culture (14-18)

Conventions: see `topic-bank.md`. m = masculine, f = feminine, pl = plural. Transliteration: ח=ch, כ=kh, צ=tz; in tables, hyphens split syllables and CAPS marks the stressed syllable.

---

## 14. Friends and small talk

**Prompts**
- L1: Meet someone at a party. Ask where they are from and what they do.
- L2: Tell a new friend how long you've been in Israel, where you live, and what you like to do on weekends.
- L3: A friend asks why you decided to move to Israel (or to study here). Explain, and ask about their experience.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| מאיפה אתה? / מאיפה את? | me-EI-fo a-TA? (m) / me-EI-fo AT? (f) | where are you from? |
| מה אתה עושה? / מה את עושה? | ma a-TA o-SE? (m) / ma AT o-SA? (f) | what do you do? |
| איפה אתה גר? / איפה את גרה? | EI-fo a-TA GAR? (m) / EI-fo AT ga-RA? (f) | where do you live? |
| כמה זמן אתה בארץ? | ka-MA za-MAN a-TA ba-A-retz? (m) / AT (f) | how long have you been in Israel? ("ba'aretz" = in the country, i.e., Israel) |
| עליתי לפני שלושה חודשים | a-LI-ti lif-NEI shlo-SHA cho-da-SHIM | I made aliyah three months ago |
| אני חדש כאן / אני חדשה כאן | ani cha-DASH (m) / cha-da-SHA (f) KAN | I'm new here |
| מה אתה אוהב לעשות? | ma a-TA o-HEV la-a-SOT? (m) / ma AT o-HE-vet la-a-SOT? (f) | what do you like to do? |
| בא לך לשתות קפה? | ba lekha lish-TOT ka-FE? (m) / ba lakh (f) | feel like having coffee? |
| מגניב | mag-NIV | cool (casual) |
| וואלה | wa-LA | really? / wow (casual) |
| מעולה / אחלה | me-u-LE / ach-LA | great / awesome (casual) |

**Common mistakes**
- "Ba li / ba lekha" (I feel like / do you feel like) is impersonal; do not conjugate it by gender of the speaker ("ba li" for everyone). Only the "to you" part changes (lekha m / lakh f).
- Gender on "ani gar/gara", "ani ohev/ohevet".
- Translating "How long have you lived here?" with a past tense; Hebrew uses present: "kama zman ata gar po?"
- Saying "ani chadash" for "I'm new" when you mean "I'm a newcomer to the country" (also fine); "oleh chadash / olah chadasha" is "new immigrant".

**Culture**
- Direct personal questions early (family, army service, salary, politics) are common and usually friendly; you can decline lightly: אני לא רוצה לענות על זה (ani lo ro-TZE / ro-TZA la-a-NOT al ze, "I'd rather not answer that").
- Conversation is lively and overlapping; interruptions are often engagement, not rudeness.
- Hosting and invitations are warm and quick ("bo e-LE-nu", come over to us).

## 15. Family

**Prompts**
- L1: Say how many siblings you have and their names.
- L2: Describe your family: parents, siblings, where they live, and what they do.
- L3: Tell an Israeli friend about a family tradition and say you miss your family; ask about theirs.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| אמא / אבא | I-ma / A-ba | mom / dad |
| אח / אחות | ACH / a-CHOT | brother / sister |
| סבא / סבתא | SA-ba / SAV-ta | grandfather / grandmother |
| בן / בת | BEN / BAT | son / daughter |
| ההורים שלי | ha-ho-RIM she-LI | my parents |
| יש לי שני אחים ואחות | yesh li SHNEI a-CHIM ve-a-CHOT | I have two brothers and a sister |
| אני הבכור / אני הבכורה | ani ha-be-KHOR (m) / ha-be-kho-RA (f) | I'm the oldest |
| נשוי / נשואה | na-SUY (m) / ne-su-A (f) | married |
| רווק / רווקה | ra-VAK (m) / re-va-KA (f) | single |
| יש לך ילדים? | yesh LE-kha ye-la-DIM? (m) / yesh lakh (f) | do you have children? |
| הם גרים בארצות הברית | hem ga-RIM be-ar-TZOT ha-BRIT | they live in the US |
| אני מתגעגע אליהם / אני מתגעגעת אליהם | ani mit-ga-GE-a (m) / mit-ga-GE-at (f) a-lei-HEM | I miss them |

**Common mistakes**
- Possession with "sheli": most nouns need "ha-" ("ha-sefer sheli", my book; not "sefer sheli"). Family words are a common exception: "ach sheli" and "ha-ach sheli" are both heard, so do not mark either as wrong.
- Counted nouns: "shnei achim" (two brothers), not "shnayim achim"; the construct form shnei/shtei goes before the noun (see `grammar-guide.md`).
- "Shel" with a name: "ha-ima shel Dan" (Dan's mother), not "ima Dan".
- Past vs present for family who live far: "hem garim" (they live), not "hayu garim" (they used to live).

**Culture**
- Family ties are central; Friday-night dinner at a parent's or grandparent's home is a common weekly ritual in many families.
- It is normal to be asked about marriage and children; light humor helps.

## 16. Weather

**Prompts**
- L1: Say it is hot today and ask what the weather will be tomorrow.
- L2: Complain about the heat and humidity and ask a friend whether it's always like this in summer.
- L3: Explain to a friend why you prefer winter to summer, and ask if they mind the rain.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| חם היום | CHAM ha-YOM | it's hot today |
| קר | KAR | cold |
| חם לי / קר לי | CHAM li / KAR li | I'm hot / I'm cold |
| יורד גשם | yo-RED GE-shem | it's raining |
| יש שרב | yesh sha-RAV | there's a heatwave (hot dry wind) |
| לח | LACH | humid |
| יש רוח | yesh RU-ach | it's windy |
| מה מזג האוויר מחר? | ma ME-zeg ha-a-VIR ma-CHAR? | what's the weather tomorrow? |
| מה התחזית? | ma ha-tach-ZIT? | what's the forecast? |
| כמה מעלות? | ka-MA ma-a-LOT? | how many degrees? (Celsius) |
| נעים | na-IM | pleasant |
| קיץ / חורף / אביב / סתיו | KA-yitz / CHO-ref / a-VIV / STAV | summer / winter / spring / autumn |

**Common mistakes**
- "Ani cham" (I am hot) means something else ("I am a hot person"); use "cham li".
- "Yored geshem" is the verb "descends", not "falls".
- Using Fahrenheit; Israel uses Celsius.
- "Ani kar li" with a double subject: just "kar li".

**Culture**
- Weather is a common opener; first rain of the season ("ha-geshem ha-rishon") is a small celebration in many places.
- A "sharav" (heatwave) is usually a topic of complaint, with advice to drink water and stay inside.

## 17. Plans and invitations

**Prompts**
- L1: Invite a friend to coffee on Thursday evening and agree on a time.
- L2: Decline an invitation politely, suggest another day, and ask where to meet.
- L3: Plan a group weekend trip over WhatsApp: propose a day, ask who is coming, delegate who brings what.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| מה אתה עושה בסוף השבוע? | ma a-TA o-SE be-SOF ha-sha-VU-a? (m) / ma AT o-SA (f) | what are you doing this weekend? |
| יש לך תוכניות? | yesh LE-kha toch-ni-YOT? (m) / yesh lakh (f) | do you have plans? |
| בא לך לבוא? | ba LE-kha la-VO? (m) / ba lakh la-VO? (f) | do you feel like coming? |
| אשמח | es-MACH | I'd be glad to (said by one person; also works as "yes, with pleasure") |
| מצטער, אני לא יכול | mitz-TA-er (m) / mitz-TA-e-ret (f), ani lo ya-KHOL (m) / ye-kho-LA (f) | sorry, I can't |
| אולי בפעם אחרת | u-LAI be-PA-am a-CHE-ret | maybe another time |
| איפה נפגשים? | EI-fo nif-ga-SHIM? | where do we meet? |
| נפגשים ב... | nif-ga-SHIM be ... | we meet at ... |
| אני בדרך | ani ba-DE-rekh | I'm on my way |
| אני כבר מגיע / מגיעה | ani kvar ma-GI-a (m) / ma-GI-a (f) | I'm about to arrive (both genders sound the same) |
| בשמחה | be-sim-CHA | gladly |
| נדבר | ne-da-BER | we'll talk (casual closing) |

**Common mistakes**
- Using "ani rotze" to accept an invitation; "ani esmach" or "besimcha" is more natural.
- Missing "ba li" impersonal: "ani ba" (I come) instead of "ba li" (I feel like).
- Gender of "yakhol/yekhola".
- Writing a very long refusal; a short, warm "mitz'ta'er, lo yakhol, u-LAI be-PA-am ha-ba-A" is fine.

**Culture**
- Plans are often made close to the time, and plans can shift; flexible timing is common ("be-seder, nitra'e"). Do not assume lateness is disrespect; confirm if timing matters.
- "Yalla" often means "let's go" or "okay"; tone matters.
- Group chat planning is normal; polls and thumbs-up emojis replace long replies.

## 18. Shabbat and holidays

**Prompts**
- L1: Greet a neighbor on Friday afternoon and wish them a good Shabbat.
- L2: You've been invited to a Friday-night dinner. Accept, ask what to bring, and thank the host.
- L3: Explain to an Israeli friend how you celebrate a holiday back home and ask how they do it here.

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|
| שבת שלום | sha-BAT sha-LOM | Shabbat shalom (Friday afternoon through Shabbat) |
| שבוע טוב | sha-VU-a TOV | have a good week (after Shabbat ends) |
| חג שמח | CHAG sa-ME-ach | happy holiday |
| שנה טובה | sha-NA to-VA | happy new year (Rosh Hashana) |
| גמר חתימה טובה | g-MAR cha-ti-MA to-VA | may you be sealed for good (Yom Kippur greeting) |
| ארוחת שישי | a-ru-CHAT shi-SHI | Friday dinner |
| תודה על האירוח | to-DA al ha-i-RU-ach | thank you for hosting |
| מה להביא? | ma le-ha-VI? | what should I bring? |
| אני אביא יין | ani a-VI YA-yin | I'll bring wine |
| מזמין אותך לשבת | maz-MIN (m speaker) / maz-mi-NA (f speaker) ot-KHA (to a man) / o-TAKH (to a woman) le-sha-BAT | I'm inviting you for Shabbat |
| חנוכה / פורים / פסח | cha-nu-KA / pu-RIM / PE-sach | Hanukkah / Purim / Passover |
| יום העצמאות / יום הזיכרון | yom ha-atz-ma-UT / yom ha-zi-ka-RON | Independence Day / Memorial Day |

**Common mistakes**
- Using "chag sameach" for every occasion; on solemn days (Yom HaZikaron, Yom Kippur, Tisha B'Av) other greetings or silence are appropriate.
- Saying "shabbat shalom" on Saturday night; after Shabbat ends use "shavua tov".
- Hosting etiquette: asking "ma lehavi?" is polite, but many hosts say "klum" (nothing) and mean it lightly; bringing something small is usually welcome.

**Culture**
- Many shops, restaurants, and public transport pause or are limited from Friday afternoon to Saturday evening; this varies by city and neighborhood. Plan ahead.
- Holiday greetings are shared widely by WhatsApp, often as forwarded images.
- Observance varies a lot, from secular to religious; ask rather than assume. Respect how a host observes Shabbat (phones, driving, cooking).
